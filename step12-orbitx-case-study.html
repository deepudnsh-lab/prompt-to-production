01 — The brief

A civilian space tourism brand
that no one has built yet.

OrbitX is the second brand in a six-brand portfolio series called "From Prompt to Production" — a self-initiated project that documents a complete, research-led web design process executed through AI direction. Every design decision is made by the designer. Every output is produced through a prompt.

The brief for OrbitX: design a hypothetical civilian space tourism brand targeting ultra-high-net-worth passengers in the Asia-Pacific market. Build a complete brand identity and a production-quality landing page. Differentiate from the three existing competitors — Blue Origin, Virgin Galactic, and SpaceX — using research, not taste.

02 — The insight that drove everything

The passenger is
invisible.

The competitor audit of Blue Origin, Virgin Galactic, and SpaceX identified one gap shared by all three sites: despite Blue Origin having flown 58 civilian passengers, Virgin Galactic having 700+ on a waitlist, and SpaceX having taken four civilians on a three-day orbital mission — not a single named civilian passenger appears on any of their homepages.

Every competitor centres the vehicle or the mission. No competitor centres the person who goes. That gap — identified through the Step 6 audit, confirmed by the Step 5 persona work — became the design thesis for the entire brand.

The central design thesis · OrbitX
Blue Origin builds the rocket. Virgin Galactic builds the experience. SpaceX builds the future. OrbitX builds the passenger.
01
The passenger is the product — not the vehicle
Every OrbitX design decision follows from this. The hero image is not the rocket — it is the view the passenger will see. The section images show the helmet visor, the person floating inside the capsule, the experience from inside. The vehicle has its own section. The passenger has every other section.
02
Arjun does not need convincing — he needs confirmation
The persona (Arjun Mehta, 52, founder, Mumbai/Dubai/Singapore) has already decided in principle. He researches before expressing interest. He reads the page on a 14" laptop at 11pm. He does not want to be sold to. The page is structured as a mission briefing document — not a hospitality experience. Facts first. Emotion emerges from precision.
03
Scarcity is real — state it as fact, not as pressure
Six seats per mission. Specific mission designations. Real launch windows. "NX-12 · Q1 2028 · 2 of 6 seats remaining" is more compelling than "Limited availability" because it is true, specific, and respects Arjun's intelligence. No urgency language. No countdown timers. Just the number.

Voice system · six rules
01 Facts before feelings
02 Never use superlatives
03 Specific over vague
04 Passenger always the subject
05 Inform. Never sell.
06 Sentences end. They do not trail.

"AT 100KM, THE ATMOSPHERE DISAPPEARS."
A fact. Not a promise. Not a tagline.
Navigation · mission document language
The Vehicle  ·  The Mission
The Training  ·  The Manifest

Not "Home / About / Experience / Book." Each label is a chapter heading in a classified briefing document. The definite article makes each section sound named and specific — as though it has always existed and is simply being referenced.
CTA language system
Primary CTA: "Reserve your manifest position →"
Secondary CTA: "View mission specifications"
Contact CTA: "Request a briefing →"
Nav CTA: "Speak to a Specialist"

None of these say "Book," "Buy," "Reserve a seat," or "Contact Us." Every word is from the register of a person being selected for a programme — not a customer completing a transaction.
05 — What this project demonstrates

What OrbitX
proves.

OrbitX is a hypothetical brand. None of the missions exist. Arjun Mehta is a composite persona. The market data is real — named and dated throughout. The design decisions are all real, and each one is traceable to a specific research finding.

01
Research as design direction
The central design thesis — "OrbitX builds the passenger" — was not an instinct. It was a finding. The competitor audit identified that the passenger is invisible across all three major space tourism sites. The persona identified what that specific passenger needs. Research produced the brief. The brief produced the design.
02
AI direction vs AI generation
Claude produced every document, every line of code, and every HTML file in this project. The design decisions — which industry, which direction, which headline, which CTA language, which images to use and how to grade them — were all made by the designer. The prompts are the creative record. The outputs are the proof that direction produces better results than generation.
03
Category intelligence as competitive advantage
The OrbitX landing page is structurally different from every competitor because the research identified specifically what every competitor does and where every competitor fails. "Left-aligned is hospitality, centre-aligned is aerospace" — that insight came from the audit, not from aesthetic preference. The research is what makes the design defensible.
04
Knowing when to change the approach
The Three.js 3D model approach was attempted, iterated, and abandoned. The decision to pivot to real video and AI-graded photography was made when the quality ceiling of the programmatic approach became clear. That decision — "the creative direction decision matters more than the technical approach" — produced a better result faster than continued iteration on the wrong tool.
05
Cross-discipline execution
Strategy (market research, persona, competitor audit) → brand identity (palette, typography, voice system) → copywriting (all eight sections, IA, CTA language) → web design (landing page build, video integration, CSS colour grading) → documentation (prompt diary, before/after, case study). One designer, one process, one complete output.
