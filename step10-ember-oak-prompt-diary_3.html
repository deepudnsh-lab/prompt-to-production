This is not a cleaned-up version of what was said. Every prompt in this document is the exact text typed during this project — unedited, including typos, mid-sentence direction changes, and single-line instructions that produced pages of output. That is the point.

The prompts reveal how a designer with 13 years of experience thinks out loud. Some are long and structured. Some are four words. Some are corrections. Some are a vision stated plainly. Step 01 · Project genesis
The original brief — starting the entire project
Direction
I want to create a new AI generated webpage portfolio for Behance. I have added my resume for context, the main aim of this project is to create very creatives, unique landing pages, which i can add to my porfolio. suggest me 5 hypothetical topics on which i can create websites (single page will do ) these 5 topic should show different types of industries. so that i can show the diversified nature of my porfolio,
What it produced
5 industry suggestions. Then "suggest 10 more" was added, then "add a kids brand as well" — the final 16-industry list emerged across three short prompts, not one long one.
What the typos reveal
"porfolio" appears twice. The prompt was not polished before sending — it was a genuine first thought. Claude responded to the intent, not the spelling. This is the correct way to work with AI: rough input, refined output.
The lesson
Start with intent, not format. "Show the diversified nature of my portfolio" is the constraint that shapes everything. The AI calibrates the quality and scope of every document to that standard.

Step 01 · Process design
Rejecting the first approach and defining the real process
Direction
no this is not right, since i want to add this to my portfolio, i want a proper flow of the whole process here is the flow that i want 1.Ideation : researching on different categories of area and selection of 5 topics 2. research on the topic 3.Creating an ideal customer profile/persona for creating website (after doing intense research on the selected topic) creating 3 create variations of mood boards for each topic 4 create content for each website based on the research 5 designing the
What it produced
The entire 14-step process framework that became the backbone of this portfolio project. The prompt was incomplete — it trailed off mid-sentence — but the structure was clear enough that Claude completed the thinking and proposed all missing steps.
The "no" at the start
Starting with "no this is not right" is the most important word in this prompt. It rejected a path before it went further. Knowing when to redirect is a design skill — it applies to directing AI exactly as it applies to directing a junior designer.
The lesson
An incomplete prompt with the right structure is more valuable than a complete prompt with the wrong structure. The numbered list — even with numbering errors — communicated the sequence. The AI filled the gaps correctly.
03
Step 01 · Quality control
Removing unverifiable claims from the project brief
Refinement
Do not add anything that is not true. Let's be factual about everything. Although this is a hypothetical situation, hypothetical case study, But based on research, this should make sense. And I do not want you to add anything that is fake. Add everything legitimate because this is my portfolio. And if I have to answer questions, I do not want to fall short.
What it produced
Removal of the line "no other Behance project in India does this" which was unverifiable. All statistics in subsequent documents were sourced with named research firms and publication years. The project became defensible in a real interview context.
Why this matters for portfolio work
"If I have to answer questions, I do not want to fall short" — this is the most important standard a portfolio can be held to. Every claim in this project was stress-tested against the question: what happens if a hiring manager asks where this number came from?
The lesson
Tell the AI your accountability standard, not just the output format. "This is my portfolio and I'll be asked about it" is a constraint that changes the quality of every subsequent document — not just this one.

Step 01 · Scope definition
Removing manual work from the process entirely
Direction
And when talking about the stack, I'm not sure that I can make a real source screen recordings. So let's remove that. Part and make sure that every step that you are included is something that you can do your on your own. I will just be there as a domain expert who can tell you what you need to do. I do not want to do any kind of manual work like recording, screen recordings, or creating something. That's the part where I want to use the full potential of AI tools So remove any part that talks about manual work
What it produced
Removal of screen recordings from the tools list. Reframing of the designer's role as "creative director and domain expert" rather than executor. Every step was confirmed to be something Claude could produce independently.
The clarity in this prompt
"I will just be there as a domain expert who can tell you what you need to do" — this is a precise role definition. It is not "let AI do everything." It is "AI executes, I direct." That distinction is the entire thesis of this portfolio.
The lesson
Define the division of labour explicitly. What will you do? What will AI do? A prompt that answers both questions produces a much better process than one that assumes the split is obvious.
05
Step 02 · Scoring criteria
Choosing the 4 scoring criteria for industry selection
Direction
Q: What 4 criteria should we score each industry against? These need to reflect what matters to you as a designer and what will make the portfolio strongest. A: Visual diversity · Persona clarity · Design challenge · Emotional range
What it produced
A complete industry scoring matrix with all 16 industries scored against these 4 criteria, totals calculated, top 6 selected, and 10 rejected with specific rationale. The criteria chosen reflected genuine design priorities — not generic business metrics.
Why these 4 criteria specifically
Visual diversity ensures the portfolio looks varied. Persona clarity ensures each site has a real audience. Design challenge ensures the work is technically interesting. Emotional range ensures different affective registers are represented. Together they produce a portfolio, not a collection.
The lesson
When the AI asks you a question, your answer is the prompt. "Visual diversity · Persona clarity · Design challenge · Emotional range" — 8 words that shaped the entire selection of 6 brands. The best prompts are sometimes answers, not questions.

Step 03 · Naming exercise
Catching a process integrity error before it went further
Refinement
One thing that I'm not sure of is that we we have already revealed the names in the first step. Is that fine to do? Or should we use, like, dummy names or placeholder names in the first step and then decide on the names in third step. Maybe by maybe after having three or four name variations for each category. You can suggest.
What it produced
Step 1 was rebuilt with industry categories only — no brand names. Step 3 was rebuilt as a genuine naming exercise with 4 candidates per brand, each using a different naming strategy, each scored and justified. The process became honest.
The instinct behind the question
This prompt came from a designer's instinct that something felt off. The brand names appearing before the naming exercise was a process integrity problem — it made the naming exercise feel like a formality. Catching this saved the credibility of the entire case study.
The lesson
Trust the instinct that something feels wrong. "I'm not sure of" is the prompt. The AI can execute a fix, but only the designer can sense when the process has a hole in it. That sense is the 13 years of experience.

Step 07 · Moodboard
Understanding what a moodboard actually is and redirecting the approach
Vision
I Like the mood board document that you created. But what it is missing is the actual mood board, which generally has a lot of images, reference images, fonts color palate etc. you gave me three options to choose from in the mood board document. Instead of creating a mood board the way I want, why don't you give me a really good prompt to create these mood board images on any other AI platform. So the end result should have 1 prompt that can create mood board image for all 3 design direction you showed me in step 7 document
What it produced
Three separate Midjourney prompts — one per direction — each specifying the 9-panel bento grid layout, the exact colour palette with hex values, the typography specimen, the atmosphere references, and the vibe word. You then ran them on Midjourney and got three full moodboard images.
The tool-switching insight
This prompt recognised that Claude produces HTML/CSS documents well but image collages are better made by image generation AI. Routing the right task to the right tool is a skill — Claude for structure and analysis, Midjourney for visual collage. The designer made that call.
The lesson
Know which AI to use for which task. Claude for reasoning, structure, and code. Midjourney for image collage. The prompt that says "give me a prompt for another platform" is a sign of a designer who understands their tools rather than expecting one tool to do everything.

Step 11 · Build
Choosing one layout concept and commissioning the final build
Build
no instead of building 3 layouts i will choose from the 3 concepts you have shared and build a final design on that please built concept 3. remember to add relevant images
What it produced
The complete Ember & Oak landing page — five full-viewport sections, Cormorant Garamond display type, ember-orange accent, real Unsplash images, custom fire cursor, scroll-reveal animations, and a working reservation form. All in a single HTML file.
The decision embedded in this prompt
"I will choose" — the selection of Concept 3 (Immersive Scroll) over Concepts 1 and 2 is a design decision. Claude proposed three structural options; the designer selected one. That selection required knowing which layout would serve the Noir direction's cinematic, full-viewport ambition best.
The lesson
Let the AI propose options. You make the selection. Generating three layout concepts costs nothing. Choosing the right one requires design judgement. The prompt "I will choose" is the clearest expression of the designer-AI relationship in this entire project.
09
Step 11 · Refinement
Three simultaneous refinements — parallax speed, dish images, fire cursor
Refinement
In the first section the hero section image at the background it taking too long to scroll up it should be faster to come to its place. i think we should add images for each dish in the menue on last thing since our heading is "where fire meets flavour" i want the mouse pointer to have some light fire effect when moving
What it produced
Three targeted fixes in one pass: parallax multiplier changed from 0.25 to 0.55 (faster scroll), dish photo thumbnails added to all 6 menu items with hover brighten effect, and the basic cursor replaced with a fire particle system — sparks spawn on mouse movement, drift upward, and fade.
The "on last thing" pattern
Three separate issues stated in sequence, each in plain language with no technical specification. "It taking too long" is enough to know the problem. "Some light fire effect" is enough to know the ambition. The AI translated intention into implementation without needing a technical brief.
The lesson
Describe the experience you want, not the code you need. "It taking too long to scroll up" is a user experience description. "Increase parallax multiplier from 0.25 to 0.55" is a technical specification. You only need to know the first one.

Step 11 · Bug fix
Diagnosing a completely broken page and requesting a full rebuild
Fix
The whole thing is broken. Don't see the mouse at all. The bottom of the page half of the page is, visible. There are no images. Check the page thoroughly and fix the issues. Also, make sure that the page is mobile friendly.
What it produced
A complete rebuild from scratch — diagnosing 4 root causes: z-index 1000 on body::after blocking all content, mix-blend-mode:screen making cursor invisible on dark backgrounds, background-attachment:fixed breaking mobile image rendering, and experience-content overflow cutting off the bottom half. All fixed in one clean file.
The value of "check the page thoroughly"
Rather than fixing one symptom at a time, this prompt asked for a comprehensive audit. The AI read the entire 1,300-line file, identified all 4 root causes, and rebuilt cleanly. A symptom-by-symptom approach would have taken 4 separate prompts and likely introduced new bugs.
The lesson
When multiple things break, ask for a thorough check, not a single fix. "The whole thing is broken" is a better prompt than "fix the cursor." It signals the scale of the problem and gives the AI permission to look at everything rather than patch one thing.

Step 11 · Final refinement
Removing the calendar icon from the date input — the 9-word fix
Fix
remove the icon in the dates section in the reservation form
What it produced
A targeted CSS fix: ::-webkit-calendar-picker-indicator { display:none } combined with appearance:none. The calendar icon was removed across Chrome, Safari, and Edge. The field still works functionally.
Why this prompt is the most instructive
9 words. No technical specification. No CSS knowledge required. The designer noticed something that looked wrong and named the thing they wanted changed. The AI knew the implementation. This is the clearest demonstration of the designer-AI division of labour in the entire project.
The lesson
You don't need to know how. You need to know what. The 13 years of experience is in knowing that a calendar icon in a premium fine dining reservation form breaks the aesthetic. The CSS that removes it is freely available. Your job is to notice the problem.

04 — The conclusion

What this diary
actually proves.

None of the prompts in this document required technical knowledge to write. None specified CSS properties, JavaScript methods, or HTML structures. Every single one described an outcome, a standard, a feeling, or a direction — and the AI translated that into execution.

What made the execution good was not the prompts. It was the judgement behind them. Knowing that brand names should not appear before the naming exercise. Knowing that a calendar icon breaks the premium aesthetic of the reservation form. Knowing that Concept 3 was the right layout for the Noir direction. Knowing when the whole thing was broken and needed a rebuild rather than a patch.

The thesis of this entire project
AI is fast. Direction is rare. This portfolio demonstrates the second one — using the first one to show it.
