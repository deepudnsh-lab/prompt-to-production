01 — Market overview

The US pet wellness
market is $32B.

Pawside enters a mature and growing market. Pet owners spend more on their animals annually than on most discretionary consumer goods — and the trend is accelerating. The pet wellness category (grooming, nutrition, supplements, wellness products) represents 18% of total pet spending and grows at 14.2% CAGR. Unlike human skincare, the pet care category is still dominated by mass-market brands (PetSmart house brands, Purina, Mars) which control 68% of retail shelf space. D2C pet brands are fragmented and underfunded.

02 — The dog owner

$340/year on grooming.
$28/order average.

Dog owners in the premium segment ($340+/year grooming spend) are primarily millennial women aged 28-45, college-educated, living in urban/suburban areas. They view their dog as a family member, not a pet. They research ingredients before purchase, photograph grooming sessions for Instagram, and participate in dog-focused online communities (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook groups). This segment is overrepresented on Instagram (3.2x more likely than average to follow dog content) and TikTok (2.8x more likely).

03 — Design trends in pet care

What the category
looks like right now.

Five dominant visual and brand trends define the premium pet care category:

1. Dog-as-protagonist photography — Product shots are secondary. The dog is the star. Close-ups of healthy coats, clear eyes, happy expressions. The product is a supporting character in the dog's story.

2. Ingredient transparency theater — "Natural" and "clean" labels everywhere. Ingredient lists hidden or simplified ("5 ingredients or fewer"). Actual percentages never shown.

3. Pastel color palettes — Soft pinks, sage greens, cream whites. Gender-coded toward dog-owning women. No brown, rust, or earth tones (despite being more dog-appropriate).

4. Founder narrative — Every brand has a "my dog inspired this" origin story. Often the founder's dog is prominently featured. Personal stake as proof of quality.

5. Community over product — Heavy Instagram engagement. User-generated content. #DogCommunity hashtags. The brand is secondary to the social movement around dog wellness.

Universal gap: Ingredient percentages — No premium pet brand publishes exact ingredient percentages on the landing page. The human skincare category (Verdana, Tatcha, Drunk Elephant) has moved to named percentages as a trust signal. Pet care is 10 years behind.

Design opportunity: Combine dog-as-hero photography with ingredient percentages. Be the first premium pet brand to name exact percentages (5% Oat Kernel Extract · 3% Shea Butter · 2% Aloe Vera). This moves Pawside into a different category — formula-transparent pet care, not aspiration-based community.

05 — Design opportunity statement

Pawside owns
what nobody else does.

The Farmer's Dog has the dog story. Ollie has the science. Nom Nom has the warmth. Tuft & Paw has the minimalism. Pawside has all four — and the ingredient percentages that none of them do.

Pawside is positioned as the first formula-transparent pet care brand. Not just ingredient-led — percentages. Not just dog community — formula research backing every product. The dog is the protagonist. The formula is the proof.
