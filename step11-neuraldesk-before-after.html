02 — Nine decisions · category average vs NeuralDesk

What changed.
Why it changed.

Before · Category average · Tableau · ThoughtSpot · Looker Studio
After · NeuralDesk · Direction C · Arctic / Clarity
01
Hero headline · the first words Marcus reads
"See and understand your data." — Tableau "Data to Decisions, Powered by Agents" — ThoughtSpot "Visualize your data for free" — Looker Studio
Category headlines describe the product or its mechanism. None describe what Marcus actually feels when he has been waiting three days for a report. The buyer's pain is invisible in every competitor headline. "Powered by AI" communicates nothing in 2026 — it is the most overused claim in SaaS.
One question. One answer. Now.
Nine words. Three sentences. No jargon. "Now" answers Marcus's primary anxiety before he has to ask. Direction C's Space Grotesk 300 at 4rem requires copy this short to breathe. It arrived in the first draft and was not changed. The word describes the experience, not the product.
02
Hero layout · alignment and first-viewport structure
Split layout — copy left, screenshot right. Left-aligned headline. Dark background. Static UI image. Single CTA.
The left-aligned split is the default SaaS hero. NeuralDesk's first build used exactly this and read as generic B2B. Direction C's minimal authority register requires full-width centred content — left-aligned split is editorial, not minimal. The first build was entirely rebuilt after this was identified.
Centre-aligned headline, rule, sub-copy, CTAs. Four live product UI screens — tab-switched below. White dominant. Red only on CTAs and data.
Centred copy reads as a statement. Left-aligned reads as a document. NeuralDesk makes a statement — then proves it with four functioning dashboard screens. The white dominant background shows the product clearly without theatrical dark-mode lighting. The product UI below the copy is more credible than any static screenshot.
03
Product demonstration · what the hero shows Marcus
Static product screenshot — frozen UI image. No interaction. No query context. "See it in action" → demo request gate.
A static screenshot shows what the interface looks like, not how it works. Marcus cannot tell what question was asked, what the answer was, or whether it works for his data. "See it in action" leading to a demo form is the interaction pattern that loses Marcus before he reads a single feature.
Four live CSS dashboard screens — tab-switched. Overview · Funnels · Channels · Retention. Animated typewriter cycling real PM questions.
Marcus can click each tab and see exactly which questions NeuralDesk answers. The typewriter cycles through questions he actually asks. The UI is built in CSS — loads instantly, no images, works offline. It demonstrates the product rather than illustrating it. No demo gate. No form. No calendar invite.
04
Pricing visibility · the decision Marcus makes in one session
Tableau: no pricing on homepage. ThoughtSpot: "Contact sales for pricing." Looker Studio: free only — no paid tiers shown.
Step 4 confirmed that every premium AI analytics competitor hides pricing behind a demo gate. "Contact sales" is an immediate exit for Marcus — it signals the product is priced above his solo approval limit and requires a calendar invite he doesn't have time for.
Free ($0) · Pro ($29/mo) · Team ($79/mo). Visible on homepage. No gate. No call. "Generate a one-pager for your manager →"
Visible pricing is a trust signal. Pro at $29/month is within Marcus's solo approval limit. The "justify to your manager" one-pager link is the most consequential line on the pricing section — it generates the ammunition Marcus needs for the Slack message to his manager that converts solo adoption to team adoption.
05
CTA language · the words on the primary button
"Get a Demo" "Start Free Trial" (requires credit card) "Contact Sales"
"Get a Demo" requires a 30-minute call Marcus doesn't have. Free trial with credit card adds friction before Marcus has seen a result. "Contact Sales" is the hardest gate. Every competitor CTA places a barrier between Marcus and the product at exactly the moment he is most likely to convert.
"Start free — no card needed" ← primary "→ See how it works" ← secondary
Two CTAs at hero level — not one. The primary removes every barrier for the ready buyer. The secondary retains the buyer who needs one more piece of evidence — keeping him on the page rather than tabbing to a competitor. The final CTA section has one button only: if Marcus has scrolled seven sections, he is ready. A second CTA there introduces doubt.
06
Social proof · how trust is built on the page
"Trusted by 500+ companies" (unverifiable) Generic star ratings — no named outcomes. "Great tool! Highly recommend." — no company.
Marcus (Step 5, Behaviour B06) is deeply skeptical of AI claims. Generic social proof has negative credibility — it signals real outcomes are unavailable. "500+ companies" is unverifiable. A five-star rating with no attribution cannot be evaluated. He tabs to the next competitor.
Metrics first: 3 hrs saved · 60s to insight · 98.4% accuracy. "We went from a 4-day turnaround to before my next standup." — Sarah K. · Senior PM · Stripe · since Jan 2026
Three specific metrics land before any testimonial — doing the credibility work first. By the time Marcus reads Sarah's quote, he has already accepted the three-hour saving as real. Every testimonial has: a full name, a job title, a recognisable company, and a specific measurable outcome. Nothing generic. Nothing that cannot be verified.
07
Copy register · technical vs outcome-led language
"Connect your data warehouse." "Query with natural language." "Build BI pipelines." "Powered by AI."
Step 4 found that all five competitors use data engineering terminology in their hero sections. "Connect your data warehouse" assumes Marcus has one — he has a Google Sheet. "Powered by AI" in 2026 communicates nothing. Marcus has been burned by this language before (Step 5, Behaviour B06) and closes the tab before reading further.
"Ask NeuralDesk anything about your data." "No analyst needed. No data warehouse required." "Your spreadsheet. A question. 60 seconds."
Written in exclusions as much as inclusions. "No SQL. No API keys. No setup call." removes the specific objections Marcus has from every previous tool. "Your spreadsheet" is the precise word that signals NeuralDesk understands its buyer — not "your data sources." His spreadsheet. The product is described in the register of the result, not the technology.
08
Visual direction · colour theme and palette choice
Dark mode. Deep navy or near-black background. Cyan, purple, or blue accent. High information density. Small type.
Step 6 confirmed: every premium AI analytics competitor — Tableau, ThoughtSpot, Power BI, and the broader AI SaaS category — defaults to dark mode. Dark mode is so dominant it is now the convention, not the differentiator. Any dark-mode AI analytics product looks like every other dark-mode AI analytics product.
White dominant (#FFFFFF). Maximum breathing room. Pure red #E8000D as the only accent colour. Space Grotesk 300 wide-tracked · IBM Plex Mono labels.
White dominant is the most visually distinctive choice in this category precisely because nobody else does it. Notion, Airtable, Loom — tools Marcus already trusts — are all light mode. NeuralDesk reads as "software I can use immediately" rather than "software that requires expertise." The pure red accent earns full visual weight because it appears so rarely.
09
Information architecture · the sequence of sections
Hero → Features → Integrations → Testimonials → Pricing (sometimes hidden) → "Get started" CTA
The category default IA is features-first, built for a buyer who needs to understand the product before they decide if they want it. Marcus is not that buyer — he decides in one session. A features-first structure wastes his 20 minutes before he reaches the information that actually matters: can I trust it, can I afford it, how do I start.
Hero → Trust strip → How it works → Product demo → Social proof → Pricing → Final CTA
Each section answers the specific question Marcus has at that moment in the scroll. Trust strip immediately after hero because his first question is "is this legitimate?" Pricing in section six — he must see the product before he reads the price. Final CTA has one button only — no credit card, no demo, just start. The IA is Marcus's evaluation session, not the product's feature list.
03 — The cumulative effect

The category plays it safe.
NeuralDesk makes a choice.

What nine research-led decisions add up to
Every competitor made the same choices: dark mode, demo gate, feature-led copy, hidden pricing, generic social proof. Those choices are not wrong — they are the accumulated wisdom of a category built for enterprise sales. NeuralDesk rejects all of them because the research identified a buyer who is not the enterprise buyer: a PM who makes a solo decision, in one session, with a company card, before his next standup. The before column is what the category built for a procurement process. The after is what research produces when you start from the actual buyer.
