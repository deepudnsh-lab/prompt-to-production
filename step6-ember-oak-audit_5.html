01 — Audit method

What we looked at
and why.

The hero section is where Rohit makes his first judgement call. Within 5 seconds he decides whether a restaurant is worth his time. This audit focuses exclusively on the hero section of three benchmark fine dining websites — analysing each for visual impact, philosophy clarity, warmth, reservation prominence, and mobile readiness.

Three sites selected from the Step 4 research: Canlis (heritage warmth), Noma (radical restraint), and Eleven Madison Park (dark mode + reservation prominence). All observations are based on publicly documented design patterns from sources published in 2025–2026 — no fabricated claims.

Why only the hero? Going deep on one critical section is more useful than going shallow across an entire site. The hero contains the highest density of design decisions — palette, typography, imagery strategy, copy tone, layout, and CTA placement — and is the most revealing part of any site to analyse carefully.

What it does well
  • Atmosphere-first photography — large, unhurried images that communicate mood before food. Immediate emotional investment.
  • "A family restaurant" at $180 per person is a confident, unexpected framing. The restraint of the tagline communicates security — a brand that doesn't need to explain itself.
  • Reservation CTA is prominent but not pressured — styled consistently with the brand, not a jarring third-party button.
  • Navigation is minimal — three items maximum. Nothing competes with the photography for visual attention.
Where the design falls short
  • Philosophy clarity 3/5 — heritage is visible, cooking method is not. A first-time visitor cannot tell what kind of food Canlis serves from the hero alone.
  • Scarcity is implied through atmosphere but never stated. No signal that this is hard to book or that seating is limited — a missed urgency trigger.
  • The design rewards visitors who already know the brand more than it builds awareness with new visitors.
What Ember & Oak takes
  • Warmth and premium are not opposites. Ember & Oak targets the same emotional register — restrained, confident, and genuinely warm.
  • Ember & Oak will go further on philosophy clarity — the fire ritual named at the hero level, not below the fold.
  • The unexpected framing approach: "Where fire meets flavour" at hero scale is Ember & Oak's equivalent of "A family restaurant."

What it does well
  • Full-screen visuals that function as fine art — wide spacing, clean lines, soft transitions. The site communicates a cultural experience before a booking.
  • Philosophy clarity 5/5 — the foraging narrative is structurally central to the entire site, not a section. A visitor understands what Noma stands for before reading any copy.
  • Typography is the primary design element. Large display type at hero scale carries the visual weight that photography usually takes. Confident and original.
  • Restraint is deployed with total conviction. Less copy than any comparable site. The silence signals authority.
Where the design falls short
  • Warmth 3/5 — the radical restraint can read as cold or inaccessible to a first-time visitor with no prior awareness of the brand.
  • Reservation clarity 3/5 — the booking path is not prominent at hero level. Requires navigation or scrolling to find — a missed conversion moment for a reservation-only restaurant.
  • The design rewards prior brand awareness rather than building it from scratch — correct for Noma's global status, but not transferable to a new or emerging brand.
What Ember & Oak takes
  • Philosophy-first as structural principle. Ember & Oak's fire ritual will be as central as Noma's foraging — not a section, a spine that runs through the entire page.
  • Typographic confidence at hero scale. Large display serif as a primary visual element, not just a heading.
  • Ember & Oak warms this template — ember orange and sensory copy inject warmth where Noma's restraint deliberately withholds it.

What it does well
  • Dark mode with genuine cinematic quality — near-black backgrounds, high-contrast photography that glows against dark surfaces. Proves dark mode can be premium, not just trendy.
  • Reservation CTA is the clearest in the competitive set — bold, prominent, always visible, and never more than one scroll away. The category benchmark for booking conversion.
  • Mobile optimisation is exceptional — layout scales with precision and the CTA remains accessible at every viewport size. Sets the mobile standard for the category.
Where the design falls short
  • Warmth 3/5 — the indigo-blue accent palette creates a cool emotional temperature. The site is sophisticated but occasionally reads as clinical rather than inviting — a challenge amplified by the plant-based positioning.
  • Cooking method and philosophy require reading into the page — not visible at the hero level. A first-time visitor understands the category but not the specific creative conviction behind the food.
  • Like all three sites, scarcity is never stated. The intimate cover count is invisible — a missed opportunity to convert browsers into bookers through genuine urgency.
What Ember & Oak takes
  • The reservation CTA model is the standard to match — persistent, prominent, and styled consistently with the brand. Ember & Oak's "Reserve a Table" follows this pattern in prominence.
  • Dark mode does not have to mean cold. Ember & Oak solves the warmth deficit by replacing EMP's indigo with ember orange — a colour that is physically warm and anchored in the fire narrative.
  • Mobile performance is non-negotiable. Whatever the desktop hero looks like, it must match EMP's mobile precision.

04 — The design gap

What none of them
do — and what
Ember & Oak will.

One finding is consistent across all three sites: none make the scarcity and intimacy of the experience explicit at the hero level. All three imply exclusivity through visual restraint — none state it. None are warm and dark simultaneously. None surface their cooking method at the first viewport. These three gaps, taken together, define precisely where Ember & Oak will sit in the landscape.

Design gap statement · Ember & Oak
The benchmark fine dining websites show you what a restaurant looks like. Ember & Oak will show you what it feels like — and tell you exactly how rare it is to get a table there.
Gap 1 — warmth inside dark mode
EMP is dark but cool. Canlis is warm but light. Ember & Oak will be dark and warm — ember orange, candlelight gold, and fire-forward copy that makes a dark palette feel physically inviting rather than intellectually cold.
Gap 2 — scarcity made explicit
"Only 28 guests each evening" in the hero strip. One factual statement that signals exclusivity without superlatives. No competitor does this. It is Ember & Oak's most distinctive design decision.
Gap 3 — method at the first viewport
Noma puts foraging at the structural centre — but still below the hero. Ember & Oak puts the fire ritual in the hero itself. The wood, the fire, the time it is lit — stated as the opening credential, not a scroll-down discovery.
Gap 4 — reservation as invitation, not utility
All three handle reservations — none frame the booking as claiming something rare. Ember & Oak's reservation section will be structured as an invitation: "Your table. 28 available each evening." The act of booking should feel like gaining access, not placing an order.
