01 · Hero headline register
"Experience transformative botanical renewal." Emotional. Aspirational. Reads as promise.
"The formula. Nothing else." Factual. Deflationary. Reads as confidence. The headline is the formula statement, not a benefit claim.
02 · Ingredient placement on homepage
Ingredient list buried on product page or footnoted as "Full INCI available." Optional information. Requires navigation to find.
Ingredient statement is section 02, immediately after the hero. The formula is the second thing Sloane sees — because it is the second thing she wants to know. No friction. No tab. No hiding.
03 · Ingredient percentages
Percentages omitted or hidden in INCI list. "5% botanical complex" — brands avoid precision because precision invites scrutiny.
Every ingredient is named and percentaged. "5% Bakuchiol · Psoralea corylifolia · Bihar, India." Precision becomes the proof of transparency. Brands that hide percentages admit they have something to hide.
04 · Hero visual register
Full-bleed lifestyle image (woman in spa, flowers, candlelight, etc). The visual is an experience promise. The product is secondary to the mood.
Product is the visual hero (single frosted glass jar on stone, centered). The packaging is the design. No lifestyle aspiration layered on top. No "this is what your life will look like" — only "this is what you are buying."
05 · Testimonial count
5–10 testimonials rotated in a carousel or grid. Volume as proof. "Thousands of 5-star reviews."
One testimonial. Elena's full quote unchanged: "I read the INCI before I ordered. I checked the bakuchiol percentage on CosDNA. I verified the squalane source. Then I bought it." One credible voice beats fifty generic ones. The testimonial is written by a buyer who reads like Sloane — because she is.
06 · Navigation architecture
Full-width sticky navbar. Stays visible. Always accessible. Maximises discoverability.
Floating pill navbar (top: 20px; border-radius: 100px; backdrop-filter: blur(32px)). Detached from the top edge. Glass effect. Intentionally smaller. The navbar does not compete with content. It is present but not dominant. Design excess avoided.
07 · Body text size
14px default. Acceptable for a website. Matches standard web convention.
16px minimum. 18px for ritual steps. Contrast ratio 4.5:1+ on all cream backgrounds. Copy is the design element — it must be read, not just present. The ingredient statements and sourcing details are not background information — they are the main content.
08 · Image treatment
Sharp corners. Flat borders. Product photography is edited to be visually perfect — product hero.
20px border-radius on every image. Box-shadow for depth. The softened edges make the minimal aesthetic feel tactile instead of cold. The product is still the hero — the rounded corners just prevent it from feeling sterile.
09 · Pricing communication
"Accessible luxury." "Without the markup." Price justified through apologetic comparison. "Worth every penny because..."
$128. No justification. No apology. No comparison. Price stated. Conversation closed. Sloane interprets price justification as insecurity. Verdana does not defend its pricing because the formula does.
03 — Why these nine matter

Each decision
solves an audit gap.

Decision 02 (ingredient placement) directly addresses the Step 6 audit finding: ingredient transparency was the universal gap. Tatcha, Drunk Elephant, and Augustinus Bader all score 1–3/5 on ingredient visibility. Verdana places the ingredient statement as the second section, removing the friction of Sloane's research journey.

Decision 04 (hero visual) responds to the moodboard discovery that ultra-minimal was more distinctive than botanical warmth. Competing on warmth alone meant competing with Tatcha. Competing on formula transparency + minimal visual design is a space Verdana owns.

Decision 01 (headline register) and Decision 09 (no price apology) both flow from the buyer insight in Step 5: Sloane does not want to be sold to. She wants to be informed. The tone of every decision reflects this — inform, don't persuade.

The nine decisions are not independent style choices. They are a coherent system responding to research. That coherence is what makes Verdana recognisable.
