01 — Audit methodology

Three sites. Five dimensions.
One territory.

Three direct competitors were audited through Marcus Webb's eyes — a Product Manager evaluating a self-serve analytics tool on a Tuesday morning with three tabs open. Each site is scored across five dimensions on a 1–5 scale. The scores are not about what each product does — they are about what each landing page communicates to Marcus in the first 30 seconds.

D
Design quality
C
Clarity of value prop
S
Self-serve ease
T
Trust signals
V
Visual differentiation

Annotation system used on all three screenshots: Green annotation = something that works well for Marcus. Amber = an observation worth noting. Red = a design gap or weakness. Screenshot annotation prompts are at the end of this document.

02 — Competitor 01

Tableau.
The category benchmark.

Tableau (Salesforce)
tableau.com · Enterprise BI · Salesforce-owned · Dark-mode hero
D: 4/5 C: 2/5 S: 1/5 T: 5/5 V: 3/5
15/25
Total score
What works
Visual production quality is the highest in the category — the hero imagery is cinematic, the typography is sharp, and the dashboard mockups are rendered at a quality that signals serious software. Salesforce's brand equity does real work here. Marcus recognises the parent company immediately, which answers the "is this legitimate?" question before it is asked. The customer logo wall is extensive and includes recognisable enterprise names.
Where it falls short
The value proposition requires prior knowledge of Tableau to decode. "See and understand your data" is the hero headline — it could describe Excel, Google Sheets, or a magnifying glass. There is no pricing anywhere on the homepage. The only conversion path is "Try Tableau" which leads to a free trial form — but the free trial requires a credit card and the pricing is revealed only after sign-up. Marcus will not get that far.
What NeuralDesk takes
The production quality standard. NeuralDesk's light theme must feel as polished and deliberate as Tableau's dark mode — every UI element, every chart mockup, every animation must read as professional software. The trust signals architecture — logos, certifications, customer count — is the right instinct, wrongly executed (no names, no outcomes, no specifics).
Design gap
Tableau assumes Marcus already knows he needs Tableau. The page is built for someone mid-evaluation, not someone discovering the category. The hero copy describes the product; it does not describe Marcus's problem. A buyer who does not already know what BI software does will not find the answer here.
03 — Competitor 02

Looker Studio.
Free but forgettable.

Looker Studio (Google)
lookerstudio.google.com · Free · Google-owned · Light UI
D: 4/5 C: 2/5 S: 5/5 T: 3/5 V: 3/5
17/25
Total score
What works
The Google brand does more trust work than any design element could. Marcus trusts Google with his data instinctively. The self-serve path is frictionless — Google OAuth sign-in, free forever, no credit card, no trial expiry. The product is genuinely accessible. The interface uses Google's Material Design system, which Marcus already knows from Gmail and Docs — zero learning curve.
Where it falls short
Looker Studio is a blank canvas. It connects data and renders charts, but it does not generate insight. There is no AI layer — it tells you what happened, not why or what to do next. The landing page design is generic Google Material — zero visual distinctiveness, no brand personality, no emotional register. A screenshot of Looker Studio is indistinguishable from Google Slides.
What NeuralDesk takes
The frictionless self-serve model. Google OAuth. Free tier. No credit card. NeuralDesk's sign-up flow should be as frictionless as Looker Studio's — one click, no form, immediate product access. The gap NeuralDesk fills is everything that happens after sign-up: the AI layer that generates insight from data rather than just rendering it.
Design gap
Looker Studio is the most accessible tool in the category and the least capable of answering a question. Marcus can connect his Google Sheets in 30 seconds — and then spend three hours trying to build a chart that tells him what he actually wanted to know. The gap between access and insight is the exact territory NeuralDesk owns.
04 — Competitor 03

ThoughtSpot.
Best design. Wrong door.

ThoughtSpot
thoughtspot.com · AI analytics · Enterprise pricing · Dark hero
D: 2/5 C: 2/5 S: 1/5 T: 4/5 V: 4/5
13/25
Total score
What works
ThoughtSpot has the best design in the category — the dark hero is visually ambitious, the natural language query demonstration is compelling, and the copy is specific enough to be credible. "Ask any question. Get instant answers." is the clearest hero headline in the category. The product screenshots show a polished interface. The customer logo wall includes recognisable names with specific outcome claims.
Where it falls short
ThoughtSpot prices at enterprise level and routes every visitor to a demo request. There is no self-serve path. Marcus clicks "Get started" and lands on a form with Company Name, Company Size, Job Title, Phone Number, and a dropdown for "What data warehouse do you use?" — he closes the tab. The product requires a data warehouse. Marcus has a Google Sheet.
What NeuralDesk takes
The natural language query demonstration at hero level. The visual quality and design ambition. The specific copy approach — outcome-led, not feature-led. NeuralDesk takes the ThoughtSpot design register and applies it to a self-serve product that Marcus can start using in 60 seconds without a data warehouse, a sales call, or a credit card.
Design gap
ThoughtSpot is the product NeuralDesk is competing with in design quality — and not competing with in sales motion. ThoughtSpot's demo gate, enterprise pricing, and data warehouse requirement make it completely inaccessible to Marcus. NeuralDesk exists in the space ThoughtSpot has vacated: premium design, self-serve access, Google Sheets as the entry point.

07 — Design opportunity statement

What the audit
confirms.

The three-site audit confirms the finding from the Step 4 research: the AI analytics category is bifurcated by a gap no single competitor has closed. Tableau and ThoughtSpot have the design quality. Looker Studio has the access. None has both — and none has the specific combination Marcus needs: premium design, self-serve access, AI-generated insight, no jargon, pricing visible, free to start.

Design opportunity · NeuralDesk · confirmed by audit
Tableau builds for the enterprise. Looker Studio builds for the data viewer. ThoughtSpot builds for the data engineer. NeuralDesk builds for Marcus — the PM who has a question, a Google Sheet, and 10 minutes before his next meeting.
