01 — The naming approach

Four candidates.
Three criteria.
One name.

Before any visual design begins, each brand needs a real name — not a placeholder. The name shapes the typographic direction, the tone of voice, and every word of copy that follows. Getting it wrong at this stage creates problems at every stage after it.

For each of the six industries, four naming candidates were generated — each using a different naming strategy. Each candidate was scored against three criteria that reflect what makes a name actually work in practice, not just sound good in a brief.

M
Memorability
Is it easy to remember, spell, and say aloud? Does it have a rhythm that makes it stick? A name that requires explanation every time is a liability.
F
Brand fit
Does the name match the emotional register of the industry? A luxury skincare brand and a kids platform need entirely different naming tones. Fit is about alignment, not cleverness.
T
Typographic strength
Does the name work as a wordmark — at favicon size and at billboard scale? Short names with varied letterforms and clean rendering score higher.

Naming strategies used: Each candidate uses one of six approaches — Evocative (a word that feels like the brand), Compound (two words paired), Descriptive (states what it is), Abstract (invented or lateral), Classical (Latin or Greek root), or Portmanteau (two words blended). Using different strategies per industry ensures the exploration is genuinely varied and not just four versions of the same idea.
