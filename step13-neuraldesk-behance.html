Project description · long (Behance project body text, paste at the start)
What this project is From Prompt to Production is a self-initiated portfolio series by Deepu Dinesh, Graphic Design Lead with 13 years of experience. Six hypothetical brands, six industries, one complete 14-step design process per brand, all executed through AI direction. NeuralDesk is Brand 04. The brief Design a complete landing page for a hypothetical self-serve AI data analytics SaaS product. The buyer is Marcus Webb — a 33-year-old Product Manager at a Series B startup in Austin, TX who loses three hours per week waiting for a data analyst who is always at capacity. The insight The AI analytics category is bifurcated: enterprise tools with high design quality and no self-serve access (Tableau, ThoughtSpot) versus self-serve tools with low design quality (Looker Studio, Polymer). No product at this moment has ThoughtSpot-quality design at a self-serve price point with pricing visible on the homepage and no demo gate. NeuralDesk occupies that territory. The design decisions Direction C — Arctic/Clarity — was selected from three Midjourney moodboard directions: ultra-minimal, white dominant, Space Grotesk 300, wide tracking, pure red #E8000D as the only accent colour. The hero is centre-aligned with four interactive product UI screens (CSS-built, tab-switched) showing Overview, Funnel Analysis, Channel Report, and Retention Cohorts — each with real metric cards, SVG charts, and an animated query bar. Fully responsive down to 320px. The process 9 steps documented · 11 verbatim prompts in the Prompt Diary · 9 before/after decisions against the category average · complete case study. All process documents built in the NeuralDesk design system: white surface, red top rule, Space Grotesk + IBM Plex Mono. Standalone files — no cross-references to other brands in the series. Tools used Claude (Anthropic) — all strategy, copy, and code Midjourney — three moodboard directions HTML / CSS / JS — no framework, no build tooling, deployable offline

04 — Image sequence · what to upload and in what order

The project
as Behance sees it.

Behance projects are image sequences — the work is presented as a series of frames that tell the process story. The order below is the correct sequence for maximum impact: lead with the final output, then show the process, then show the detail.

01
Cover image — landing page hero, full width, 1400×900px
Screenshot the NeuralDesk landing page hero at 1400px wide. The hero with the tab-switched UI panel and the centred headline is the most visually striking frame. This is the Behance thumbnail — it must stop the scroll.
02
Full landing page scroll — composite at 1440px wide
Screenshot the full page as a tall composite: hero → logo strip → how it works → product bento → social proof → graphs → pricing → final CTA. Shows the complete design system applied end-to-end. Export as a single tall PNG, not individual section screenshots.
03
Mobile responsive — three device sizes side by side
Show the landing page at 375px (iPhone), 640px (mobile breakpoint), and 960px (tablet). Arrange the three frames side by side on a white background. This demonstrates the responsive implementation which is a significant technical deliverable.
04
Four product UI screens — tab switcher detail
Screenshot each of the four product UI panels (Overview, Funnels, Channels, Retention) at full size. Arrange as a 2×2 grid on white. This shows the depth of UI craft — each screen has real charts, real metric cards, real data.
05
Moodboard — Direction C · Arctic / Clarity
The Midjourney Clarity moodboard image. Full width. Shows the visual direction that the entire landing page was built to match. Add a small caption: "Direction C — Arctic / Clarity · Selected at Step 07."
06
All three moodboard directions — comparison
Signal · Momentum · Clarity arranged in a 3-column layout on white. Shows the creative range and the deliberate selection of Direction C over two strong alternatives.
07
Design system — palette, type, components
A composed frame showing: the 8-colour palette strip, the Space Grotesk 300 type specimen ("One question. One answer. Now."), the IBM Plex Mono data label, and the primary red CTA button. White background, clean layout.
08
Competitor audit — annotated screenshots
The three annotated competitor screenshots (Tableau, Looker Studio, ThoughtSpot) side by side. Shows the research rigour behind the design decisions. Caption: "Step 06 — Competitor Visual Audit · Three sites scored across 5 dimensions."
09
Before & After — two decisions side by side
Select the two most impactful before/after comparisons (hero layout, visual direction) and present them as a clean 2-column frame. Label each clearly. Shows that the design is research-led, not taste-led.
10
Process overview — the 9-step timeline
A clean summary frame listing all nine steps with their status (Complete / Key step). Shows the breadth of the process — that the landing page is the output of a documented strategic process, not a one-session design exercise.
