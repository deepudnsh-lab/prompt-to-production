Step 04 — Industry Research
Market sizing · competitor landscape · design trends
Worked first time
Prompt sent to Claude
Brief locked. Building Step 4 now. Verdana — Brand 05 of 06 - Market: US clean beauty · millennial/Gen Z · $80–$300 - Direction: Warm & botanical · cream · sage · earthy · ingredient-forward - Buyer: Woman · 25–35 · aesthetic-led · texture and packaging first
What worked
The three-line brief locked the market, direction, and buyer before the first document was built. Market data returned well-sourced — Grand View Research (Jan 2026), Mintel 2025, Euromonitor 2025. The competitor scoring table (Tatcha 19/25, Drunk Elephant 17/25, Augustinus Bader 16/25) arrived cleanly on the first pass. The design opportunity statement — "Tatcha has the ceremony. Drunk Elephant has the science. Augustinus Bader has the authority. Verdana has all three" — arrived verbatim in the first draft and was not changed.
What needed fixing
Nothing significant on the first pass. The only adjustment was a request to reconcile market segment figures — some cited 2024 base year data, others 2025. Correction applied: verify all stats use consistent base year and flag any variance. This is the same error that appeared in OrbitX Step 4 — base year consistency must be specified upfront.
Lesson
A three-line brief — market, direction, buyer — is the minimum viable brief for a research document. It constrains the output without over-specifying it. The design opportunity statement wrote itself once those three constraints were in place. Always lock these three before asking for research.

Step 05 — Customer Persona
Sloane Mitchell · UX Designer · 29 · Brooklyn NY
One iteration
Prompt sent to Claude
lets use an american persona Quick update to the persona — keeping everything else identical, just shifting Vikram to an American buyer with the right market context. Update persona name, location and currency references to American context
What worked
The correction was quick and precise — name, location, and currency updated without losing any of the behaviour detail. Sloane Mitchell, 29, Brooklyn, $94K, $340/month skincare spend emerged as a credible and specific buyer. The six behaviours section arrived strongly on the first pass — especially B04 (photographs every product before using it) and B06 (treats skincare as an investment, not an indulgence). The buyer thesis — "Sloane does not choose between beautiful and effective. She requires both." — arrived in the first draft unchanged.
What needed fixing
The first draft used an Indian buyer name (Vikram) which was inconsistent with the US clean beauty market brief. The correction — "lets use an american persona" — was the entire prompt. It was enough. The lesson: the persona document inherits the market constraint from Step 4. If Step 4 specifies US market, Step 5 should auto-apply US context. The fact that it did not is a reminder to always restate the market in the persona prompt explicitly.
Lesson
Always restate the target market at the top of the persona prompt — do not assume it carries over from Step 4. "US market, American buyer" takes four words and prevents a nationality mismatch that requires a correction round. The same error appeared in the NeuralDesk persona (Vikram → Marcus Webb). This is a recurring pattern worth catching at the brief stage.

Step 06 — Competitor Visual Audit
Tatcha · Drunk Elephant · Augustinus Bader
Worked first time
Prompt sent to Claude
good, do the next step now
What worked
The five-dimension scoring system (D · I · A · S · W) produced a genuinely useful comparison. The W (Warmth & Botanicals) score was the revelation — all three competitors scored 1 or 2. This confirmed the audit finding from Step 4: the warmth gap is not theoretical, it is visually absent from every competitor homepage. The three annotation prompts were specific enough to produce annotated screenshots usable on the first generation. The universal gap table was the strongest output in the document.
What needed fixing
The annotated screenshots came back with scores that differed from the initial estimates in the document. Tatcha's actual annotation scored W: 5/5 (not 2/5 as initially estimated) — the warm pastel palette reads as warm even if the brand origin is Japanese, not botanical. Scores were updated in the document to match exactly what the annotations showed. Always update the document scores to match the actual annotated output — not the pre-annotation estimate.
Lesson
Pre-score estimates before annotation are useful for structuring the document, but they must be treated as provisional. The annotation is the truth — the document must be updated to match it. In Verdana's case, the W score discovery (Tatcha 5/5 warmth) actually sharpened the brief: Verdana needs warmth AND ingredient transparency, because Tatcha already has the warmth. The combination is what Verdana uniquely owns.

Step 07 — Moodboard · Direction selection
Three directions · Direction C Still selected
Key decision
Direction lock prompt — verbatim
Q: Which direction are you locking for Verdana? A: Direction C — Pared-back / Still · cream · stone · ultra-minimal · formula-first
What worked
All three Midjourney moodboards were usable on the first generation. The bento-box grid instruction ("9 panels at 16:9 landscape ratio, like a real designer's client presentation") produced structured, professional outputs across all three directions. Direction C (Still) — the single frosted glass jar on marble, "THE FORMULA. NOTHING ELSE." in wide-tracked caps, the ingredient label close-up "5% Bakuchiol · 2% Squalane" — was immediately the most distinctive against the competitor set. Direction C was the most counterintuitive choice given that warmth was Verdana's stated differentiator — and it was the right one.
What needed fixing
Direction A (Verdure) and Direction B (Ritual) both read as category-expected for premium clean beauty. Beautiful executions, but not differentiated from Youth to the People or Tatcha at a visual level. The choice of Direction C was the correction — moving to ultra-minimal when the audit showed that warmth was already crowded territory. The moodboard exercise revealed that the least obvious direction was the most distinctive one.
Lesson
When all three moodboard directions are strong, choose the one that is most unexpected given the category. Direction A (botanical warmth) was what every brief for a clean beauty brand would produce. Direction C (pared-back minimal) was what none of them had. The audit gap is not always the most visible gap — it is sometimes the gap left by every competitor's assumption about what the category must look like.

Step 07 — Midjourney Prompts
Direction C · Still · as used verbatim
Worked first time
Midjourney prompt · Direction C (Still) — as used
Create a professional brand moodboard for a premium US clean beauty skincare brand called Verdana with an ultra-minimal, ingredient-first aesthetic, targeting design-literate millennial women aged 25–35. Layout: bento-box grid collage of 9 panels at 16:9 landscape ratio, like a real designer's client presentation. Include: a single frosted glass skincare jar on a marble surface — nothing else in the frame, maximum white space, soft morning light, close-up of a cream ingredient list printed in DM Mono typeface on warm cream paper — "5% Bakuchiol · 2% Squalane · 0.5% Phytol" — clean, nothing decorative, an abstract photograph of still water on a stone surface — cool sage green tones, extreme calm, a product flatlay — one jar, one dropper, linen cloth, one sprig of sage — nothing extra, a colour palette strip showing Warm White #FAF7F2 · Off-White #F5F0E8 · Stone #D8D0C4 · Forest Sage #5C7A5F · Muted Umber #8A7A6A · Deep Ink #1A1612, typography specimen "The formula. Nothing else." in ultra-light wide-tracked sans-serif on warm cream, a macro photograph of a single drop of serum on a stone surface catching morning light — no other elements, a close-up of the product label — just the brand name and ingredient percentages, clean sage type on cream, and the word "Still" large in ultra-light wide-tracked uppercase in forest sage on warm cream. --ar 16:9 --style raw --v 6
What worked
The moodboard generated correctly on the first attempt. Naming the hex codes in the prompt caused Midjourney to include a colour palette strip — the single most professional detail in the output. "Like a real designer's client presentation" set the quality register accurately. The ingredient label panel ("5% Bakuchiol · 2% Squalane · 0.5% Phytol" in DM Mono on cream) was the most distinctive panel — it made the moodboard feel like an actual Verdana design artefact, not just mood reference imagery.
What needed fixing
Direction B (Ritual) came back slightly too dark — the apothecary workspace felt more gothic than premium. The fix: "luxury design agency presentation" added to the prompt. Direction A (Verdure) was clean on the first generation. Direction C (Still) required no regeneration — the extreme minimal brief ("nothing else in the frame," "maximum white space") translated directly into the correct visual register.
Lesson
For ultra-minimal directions, exclusions in the Midjourney prompt are as important as inclusions. "Nothing else in the frame," "no other elements," "nothing extra" — these negative instructions are what produces the stillness. Without them, the model adds context objects to fill space. The minimal aesthetic requires explicit instruction to remain minimal. Hex codes in the palette strip instruction make the output immediately usable as a design artefact.

Step 08 — Copywriting · IA decision
Seven sections · formula-first structure
One iteration
Prompt sent to Claude

What worked
The seven-section IA was correct on the first pass — each section mapped exactly to a question from Sloane's purchase journey documented in Step 5. The hero headline "The formula. Nothing else." arrived in the first draft and was not changed. The ingredient statement as the second section (not the fifth) was the most important structural decision — placing it where Sloane goes immediately after the hero. Elena's testimonial ("I read the INCI before I ordered. I checked the bakuchiol percentage on CosDNA.") was the strongest single piece of copy in the document.
What needed fixing
The six voice rules section in the first draft included a rule that referenced how Verdana differs from "conventional skincare brands." Required correction: remove comparative framing. A brand's copy document should define itself on its own terms — not by contrast to a generic category. This is the same error that appeared across OrbitX and NeuralDesk Step 8 documents. The pattern is consistent: Claude defaults to contrast framing; it must be caught and removed every time.
Lesson
Add "no comparative framing — define on own terms only" to every Step 8 prompt as a standard closing instruction. This prevents the recurring error of Claude contextualising a brand by what it is not rather than what it is. The voice rule about not apologising for price — "state the price, move on" — is the most important single rule for a premium brand. It should be rule 01, not rule 03.

Landing Page — Image strategy
11 images · single master prompt · zip delivery
Key decision
Prompt sent to Claude — the correction that changed the image workflow
No. I don't want to add these prompts individually. I want one single prompt that I can copy paste in any of these image creating AI tools. And this one single prompt should be able to all the images that you need and zip them and give me a downloadable zip folder. Rewrite the prompt. And share it with me.
What this fixed
The original approach produced 11 individual prompts — one per image — which required 11 separate copy-paste operations. The correction produced a single master prompt that instructs the AI tool to generate all 11 images, name them correctly, and package them as a downloadable zip. This is the correct workflow for any brand that requires multiple images — one master prompt, one generation session, one download. ChatGPT (GPT-4o with image generation) handles this end-to-end including the zip creation.
What the original approach missed
The first version of the image brief had individual prompts with individual filenames — useful as documentation but impractical as a workflow tool. A designer working across 6 brands cannot spend 45 minutes copy-pasting 11 prompts per brand. The master prompt approach reduces the image generation workflow to: copy once, paste once, download once. The brief becomes a tool, not a reference document.
Lesson
Every image brief for future brands should be a single master prompt, not a collection of individual prompts. The master prompt includes: global style rules once at the top, then all image descriptions with filenames, then one closing instruction to package as a zip. This is the standard workflow for Steps 4–14 brands 06 onward. The designer's time is the constraint — the prompt structure should minimise the number of manual actions required.

Landing Page — Visual direction
Body text · contrast · border radius · glassmorphism
One iteration
Prompt sent to Claude
the body font is too small to read and lacks contrast, i also think we should have a corner radius of 20 for all images. also use glass morphism where ever you can, i would like to see it especially on the navbar, instead of a full width nav bar lets create smaller floating navbar in glass effect
What worked
The floating pill navbar — `position:fixed; top:20px; left:50%; transform:translateX(-50%); border-radius:100px; backdrop-filter:blur(32px)` — immediately separated Verdana from a standard full-width nav. Glass effects applied to: hero eyebrow pill, face cell overlays (frosted glass card replacing the dark gradient), proof metric cards, ingredient note pill, sourcing detail pill, and final CTA badges. The 20px border radius on all images softened the ultra-minimal aesthetic — making the product shots feel tactile rather than sterile.
What needed fixing
Body text was 14px with umber `#8A7A6A` — readable on white but too light for a brand whose copy carries significant weight. Corrected to 16px and umber darkened to `#5C4E42`. The ingredient source notes at 11.5px were also too small — corrected to 14px. The contrast gap was most visible on the ritual steps section where the 13px step text at `#4A4038` was difficult to read on the cream2 background.
Lesson
For a brand where copy is a primary design element — where ingredient percentages and sourcing specifics must be read, not just seen — type size and contrast are not finishing details, they are functional requirements. 16px minimum for all body text. Contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for any text on cream backgrounds. Check both on the actual background colour — umber that reads well on white can fail on cream. The glassmorphism floating navbar is now standard for Verdana and Brand 06.

Landing Page — Continuing build
Reconnecting after a session break
Session continuation
Prompt sent to Claude
lets continue from where we left off
What worked
The memory system and project context allowed seamless continuation — the landing page state, all 11 embedded images, and all visual updates from the previous session were preserved. The file was located, verified (11 images, all 9 sections present), and shared without any re-briefing required. The project memory across sessions means Verdana's design decisions compound — each session builds on the last without re-explaining context.
What needed fixing
Nothing needed fixing in the session continuation itself. The pattern to note: "lets continue from where we left off" is a valid prompt but requires the AI to reconstruct state from memory and file checks. The more reliable approach is to specify which step is next: "lets do step 10 — prompt diary." This removes ambiguity and produces a faster, more focused response.
Lesson
When resuming a long project across sessions, specify the next step explicitly rather than asking the AI to determine where you left off. "Continue from where we left off" works when the project state is clear; "Let's do Step 10 — Prompt Diary" works every time. The step number is the fastest possible context signal — it maps directly to the 14-step process and leaves no ambiguity about what comes next.

The single instruction that built the page
The briefest prompt
The prompt — 8 words that built the landing page
lets build the landing page, also please remember that since this is a skin care brand we we will have to add really detailed mock up of the products and also really good images of beautiful faces, so for all the images that we will be using in this landing page i want you to give me a single prompt that can create all these images and add them in a zip file for me to easily download(you should also be able to read this zip file) I will use this prompt on any other image creating AI tool like Gemini or Chat gpt.
What this produced
A single prompt built the complete landing page structure (9 sections, all copy from Step 8, all placeholder zones for 11 images), generated the image manifest as a downloadable zip, and established the master image prompt workflow. The instruction to include "detailed mockups of products and really good images of beautiful faces" was the key creative direction — it elevated the brief from "build a landing page" to "build a luxury skincare landing page that looks like a magazine editorial."
What needed clarifying
The original image brief produced 11 individual prompts. The correction — "I want one single prompt that I can copy paste in any of these image creating AI tools" — was the key iteration. The master prompt approach is significantly more efficient: one copy operation versus eleven. The zip file delivery adds another layer of efficiency — images arrive pre-named and ready to embed. This workflow is now standard for Brand 06.
Lesson
For a skincare brand (or any brand where photography is the primary design element), the image brief is as important as the copy brief. "Really detailed mockups" and "really good images of beautiful faces" — two phrases — set the quality register for the entire landing page. Without this specificity, the image brief produces generic stock-photo-level outputs. With it, the outputs are editorial-quality and brand-specific. Specificity of expectation produces specificity of result.
03 — Three key lessons · apply to all six brands

What Verdana
taught the series.

Lesson 01 — On direction selection
When all three moodboard directions are strong, choose the most unexpected one. The audit gap is not always the most visible gap — it is sometimes the gap left by every competitor's assumption about what the category must look like. Direction C (ultra-minimal) was counterintuitive for a warmth-differentiator brand. It was the right choice precisely because it was counterintuitive.
Lesson 02 — On image workflow
Every image brief should be a single master prompt. Not a collection of individual prompts. One copy, one paste, one download. The master prompt includes global style rules once, all image descriptions with filenames, and one closing instruction to package as a zip. This is the standard workflow for Brand 06 onward.
Lesson 03 — On copy as a design element
For a brand where copy carries the primary design weight — where ingredient percentages must be read, not just seen — type size and contrast are functional requirements, not finishing details. 16px minimum. 4.5:1 contrast ratio minimum. Check both on the actual background colour. The glassmorphism floating navbar and 20px image radius are now standard for the remaining brand in this series.
