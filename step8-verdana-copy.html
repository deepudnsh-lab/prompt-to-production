01 — Voice rules

How Verdana
speaks.

All copy is written before any layout decision. Direction C's Still register demands copy that matches the visual restraint — specific, quiet, and completely uninterested in persuading anyone who has not already arrived. Six rules govern every word.

Rule 01 · Formula before feeling
Lead with what is in the jar. Name it. Percentage it. Source it.
Experience the transformative power of nature's finest botanicals.
Sloane reads the INCI list. She will know if the copy and the formula disagree. Lead with the truth.
Rule 02 · Specific over evocative
5% bakuchiol. Cold-pressed. Psoralea corylifolia. Nothing added.
Nature's answer to retinol. Gentle yet powerful. Deeply transformative.
Every claim must be verifiable. "Deeply transformative" cannot be tested. "5% bakuchiol" can.
Rule 03 · No price apology
$128. The Botanical Cream.
Accessible luxury. Without the markup. Worth every penny.
Sloane interprets price justification as insecurity. State the price. Move on. The formula justifies itself.
Rule 04 · Short sentences. No trails.
The formula. Nothing else.
A carefully crafted formula that works in harmony with your skin's natural renewal cycle to gently...
Direction C's wide-tracked Cormorant italic reads best in declarative fragments. Long sentences fight the aesthetic.
Rule 05 · Never claim "natural"
"Bakuchiol from Psoralea corylifolia, cold-pressed within 48 hours of harvest."
100% natural. Made with nature's ingredients. Clean and green.
"Natural" is the most distrusted word in clean beauty. Name the ingredient, name the source, name the process. That is the proof.
Rule 06 · Standalone document
This document is complete and self-contained.
Unlike conventional skincare brands, Verdana…
No comparative framing in brand copy. Verdana defines itself on its own terms. The formula speaks for itself.
02 — Information architecture

Seven sections.
Seven things Sloane needs to see.

The landing page is structured as Sloane's purchase evaluation — from the moment the packaging stops her scroll to the moment she adds to cart. Each section answers the specific question she has at that point in the journey documented in Step 5.

SectionNameSloane's questionCopy objective
01HeroIs this the brand that understands what I want?The formula. The vibe. Nothing else. Win in 5 seconds.
02Ingredient statementWhat is actually in it? At what percentage?Named ingredients, named percentages, named sources. Above the fold.
03The ritualWhat does using this feel like? Will it fit my morning?Two sentences. Texture. Time. That is all.

03 — Complete copy · all seven sections

Every word.
Before any layout.

01
Hero — the first thing Sloane sees
Hero
Botanical skincare · ingredient-first · $80–$180
The formula.
Nothing else.
Three botanicals. Named percentages. Cold-pressed within 48 hours of harvest. No fragrance. No fillers. Nothing you did not ask for.
Begin the ritual
→ Read the formula
Copy decisions
"The formula. Nothing else." — four words, a full stop, and a line break. Direction C's Cormorant Garamond 300 italic at 4rem requires copy this spare. The period after "formula" is not a stylistic choice — it is a statement of completeness. The "nothing else" answers Sloane's first suspicion: what are you hiding? Nothing. The sub-copy uses exclusions as much as claims — "No fragrance. No fillers. Nothing you did not ask for." This is the voice pattern that earns Sloane's trust fastest.
Why this structure
Two CTAs — "Begin the ritual" for the buyer who is ready, "Read the formula" for the buyer who needs one more piece of evidence. "Begin the ritual" is not "Shop now" or "Add to cart" — it frames the purchase as the start of something, not the completion of a transaction. This is the language Sloane uses about her own routine ("I do my ritual every morning") and it signals that Verdana understands her relationship with skincare.
02
Ingredient statement — the most important section on the page
Ingredients
What is in the Botanical Cream
5% Bakuchiol Psoralea corylifolia · cold-pressed · Bihar, India
2% Squalane Sugarcane-derived · not shark liver · COSMOS certified
0.5% Phytol Chlorophyll-derived · stimulates collagen synthesis
93.5% Base Aloe vera, shea butter, jojoba — no fragrance, no silicones, no parabens
Full INCI list available on every product page. No exceptions.
Copy decisions
The ingredient statement is a structural element of the homepage — not a footnote buried on a product page. Every ingredient is named, percentaged, and sourced. The sourcing detail ("Bihar, India · cold-pressed") is the evidence that Verdana knows its supply chain. The squalane source note ("not shark liver") answers a specific concern Sloane has researched — it proves the brand reads the same communities she does. "Full INCI list available on every product page. No exceptions." is the transparency commitment stated plainly.
Why this placement
Sloane's purchase journey (Step 5, Stage 02) goes directly to ingredient investigation after the hero. By placing the ingredient statement as the second section, Verdana removes the friction of that journey. She does not need to open INCIDecoder. She does not need to hunt for the product page. The formula is the second thing she sees — because it is the second thing she wants to know. This is the most direct possible response to the audit finding that ingredient transparency is the universal gap across all three competitors.
03
The ritual — texture and time
Ritual
The ritual
Two minutes. Every morning.
Press three drops into clean skin. The texture absorbs completely — no residue, no shine. You will not notice it is there. Your skin will.
Copy decisions
"Two minutes. Every morning." — the ritual section answers the texture question and the time question simultaneously, in the first line. Sloane buys on texture (Step 5, B02) — "The texture absorbs completely — no residue, no shine" is a specific sensory claim she can verify on first use. The final line ("You will not notice it is there. Your skin will.") is the only moment of emotional register in the section — earned by everything specific that preceded it.
Why only two sentences of description
Direction C's pared-back register means the ritual section earns its restraint. A 200-word description of "how to apply" is a wellness brand move, not a premium brand move. Premium brands trust their buyer to know how to use a serum. What Sloane needs from this section is confirmation that the texture will work for her routine ("no residue, no shine") and permission to trust the product without being guided through it like a tutorial.

Copy decisions
Each product is described in one line — the key percentages, nothing else. The product name and the price appear before the description. Price before description signals confidence — Verdana does not bury the number to avoid the conversation. Each product description follows the same format: percentages, then one outcome phrase. "For visible tone." "To close the routine." These are functional, not aspirational — they tell Sloane what the product does in the routine, not how it will make her feel.
Why three products only
The Step 4 trend analysis identified "slow beauty — fewer products, deeper rituals" as an emerging dominant trend in the $80–$300 tier. Three products signals curation, not limitation. Sloane is not looking for 40 SKUs — she wants a complete routine she can trust and repeat. Three products maps exactly to her behaviour (Step 5): she has six products from four brands, suggesting she needs two products per brand to feel the brand is complete. Three gives her one extra product of trust.
05
Social proof — one testimonial, ingredient-literate
Social proof
"I read the INCI before I ordered. I checked the bakuchiol percentage on CosDNA. I verified the squalane source. Then I bought it. Six weeks in — my skin barrier is the strongest it has been in years. I have stopped looking for the next thing."
Elena R. · 31 · product designer, Portland · using Verdana since October 2025
Copy decisions
The testimonial is written in Sloane's exact voice. "I read the INCI before I ordered" — this is how Sloane shops. "I checked the bakuchiol percentage on CosDNA" — this is what Sloane does. The testimonial works because it describes the exact purchase behaviour from Step 5 and then delivers a specific, time-bounded outcome ("six weeks in") with an emotional payoff that is not hyperbolic ("I have stopped looking for the next thing"). Elena is 31, a product designer, in Portland — Sloane's demographic and professional world exactly.
Why only one testimonial on the homepage
Three testimonials is the SaaS model. One testimonial is the luxury model. Augustinus Bader, La Mer, and Tatcha do not stack six testimonials in a carousel — they present one curated voice with authority and let it carry the weight. Direction C's minimal aesthetic reinforces this: a wall of five-star reviews would be visually and tonally wrong. One testimonial from someone exactly like Sloane, saying exactly what Sloane would say, is more persuasive than fifty generic ones.
06
The sourcing — where the botanicals come from
Sourcing
The source
Bihar, India. October harvest.
Our bakuchiol is sourced from a single cooperative farm in Bihar, India — where Psoralea corylifolia grows in the monsoon season and is harvested once annually. Cold-pressed within 48 hours. No solvent extraction. The 5% concentration in our Botanical Cream reflects the highest evidence-backed threshold for visible skin renewal without irritation.
Supply chain documentation available on request.
Copy decisions
"Bihar, India. October harvest." — the section headline names a place and a time. This is the most specific possible claim about sourcing and it signals immediately that Verdana knows exactly where its ingredients come from. The body paragraph explains the process ("cold-pressed within 48 hours"), the method exclusion ("no solvent extraction"), and the formula decision rationale ("the highest evidence-backed threshold") — all in four sentences. "Supply chain documentation available on request" is the final trust signal: the evidence exists and Verdana will share it.
Why sourcing gets its own section
Sloane (Step 5, B03) uses INCIDecoder and CosDNA to verify ingredients she cannot decode herself. The sourcing section pre-empts her next research step. By telling her where the bakuchiol comes from and how it is processed, Verdana answers the question she would otherwise spend 20 minutes researching independently. Brands that answer research questions before they are asked convert buyers who would otherwise tab away to verify. This section turns Sloane's due diligence from a risk into a confirmation.
07
Final CTA — one action, one line of reassurance
CTA
When you are ready
Begin the ritual.
Shop the collection
Free shipping over $80 · Returns within 30 days · No account required
Copy decisions
"When you are ready" — not "Don't miss out" or "Limited quantities." Verdana does not create urgency because urgency is incompatible with the premium register. The buyer who has scrolled through six sections of ingredient specificity and sourcing documentation is already decided — she does not need pressure. "Begin the ritual" restates the hero CTA language and closes the loop. The reassurance line answers the three remaining objections in one line: shipping (free over $80 — she is spending $128), returns (30 days), account creation (not required).
Why one button only
The hero section has two CTAs because Sloane may not be ready at arrival. The final section has one because if she has read the ingredient percentages, the sourcing origin, and the testimonial from Elena — she is ready. A second CTA ("Or book a consultation" / "Try a sample first") introduces doubt at the critical moment. Direction C's pared-back aesthetic makes this especially true: a second button in this context reads as uncertainty. One button. Centred. Quiet. Confident.
04 — A note on this document

Formula first.
Always.

Every word above was written before any layout decision. The information architecture was designed first — Sloane's seven questions, in the sequence she asks them. Then the copy was written to answer each question. Then the visual preview was added to show how Direction C's Pared-back / Still aesthetic frames each section.

The pared-back register of Direction C makes copy quality more visible than in any other design direction — there is nowhere for weak copy to hide. When the page has almost no decoration, every word is exposed. The ingredient percentages, the sourcing specifics, and the testimonial from Elena must earn their place or the page collapses into blankness.
