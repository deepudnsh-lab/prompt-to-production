Full project description · paste into Behance project overview
The project Bumblebee World is Brand 03 in From Prompt to Production — a self-initiated case study series demonstrating a complete web design process executed through AI direction. I am the creative director and domain expert. Claude (Anthropic) and Gemini are the execution engines. The brief Design a landing page for a hypothetical Indian children's books and toys brand targeting early learners aged 3–6. The buyer is an urban, aspirational Indian mother — not the child. Every design decision must pass two simultaneous tests: the child's engagement test and the parent's Instagram test. The brand must be beautiful AND Indian AND educational — something no competitor in the category achieves simultaneously. The process 9 steps, each documented as a standalone HTML file. Industry research with sourced statistics (₹4,200 Cr books market, ₹18,500 Cr toys market, 62% gifting purchase rate). A persona built from real demographic data. A competitor visual audit across Usborne, Kiddopia, and Tulika Books. Three AI-generated moodboard directions on Midjourney. Complete copywriting before any layout decision. A multi-tool landing page build — Claude for iterations, Gemini for the forest illustration system and illustrated book covers, Claude again for brand alignment and image embedding. The design direction Direction A — Sunny / Bloom. Sunshine #FFD000 as the primary palette signal — the colour of marigolds, school bags, turmeric, ripe mangoes. Nunito 900 for display. DM Mono for labels. Forest parallax illustration system. Illustrated book cover mockups with Indian characters. Base64-embedded images. Dark/light theme toggle. Hero headline: "Every child is a world." The multi-tool workflow Brand 03 introduced a new workflow to the series: a Gemini-generated HTML file (forest illustrations, book cover art, dark/light toggle) brought back to Claude for brand alignment, palette correction, and content replacement. Documented in full in Step 10. Tools used Claude (Anthropic) · Gemini · Midjourney · Google Fonts · Pure HTML / CSS / JS

03 — Cover image specification

The thumbnail
is everything.

On Behance, 80% of click decisions are made from the thumbnail. The cover image must communicate the brand's visual identity, quality level, and category in under two seconds. The wrong cover loses the click before anyone reads a word.

What to screenshot
The hero section of bumblebee-landing.html — the forest illustration with the "Every child is a world." headline and the trust badge strip at 1400px wide. This is the single strongest visual in the project. It communicates the brand palette, illustration system, Indian identity, and quality level simultaneously.
Every child
is a world. 🌻
Sunshine · Leaf · Marigold · Cream
Specification
Size: 1400 × 900px minimum · Format: JPG, quality 90+ · Crop: Hero section only, no scrolled content below the fold · Browser: Chrome at 1400px viewport width · Theme: Light theme (forest illustration is most visible)
Why not a process document
Process documents are what you show after someone clicks. The cover earns the click. The forest illustration hero — warm yellow, Indian characters, professional illustration quality — stands out against the dark-mode OrbitX and charcoal Ember & Oak covers in the series. The series covers should be visually diverse. Bumblebee World's sunshine yellow is the most distinctive thumbnail in the set.

How to frame the series on Behance: Each brand is a separate Behance project. All six share the "From Prompt to Production" series name and the same document design system — so a recruiter who clicks one project immediately recognises it as part of a larger body of work. Cross-link all published projects to each other in the description. The series framing is what turns three individual projects into a portfolio statement. When all six are live, it becomes a complete demonstration of design range across six industries, six visual registers, and six completely different design problems.

The series thesis — what all six brands together demonstrate
A design lead who can direct AI across six industries, six visual registers, and six completely different design problems — with the same rigour, the same process, and the same standard of documentation each time. Not "I used AI to make things faster." Rather: "I used AI to demonstrate a level of process rigour and design range that would take a team of five to produce manually — and I directed every decision."
