01 — What goes on Behance

How to present Pawside.

Behance is a portfolio platform for creative professionals. The Pawside project will be presented as part of the "From Prompt to Production" series — six hypothetical brands, each built through a 14-step process using AI as the execution engine.

The presentation strategy: show the full methodology, not just the final design. Viewers should understand the thinking, the process, the decisions, the iterations. This is a case study in collaborative design, not a finished brand pitch.

03 — Design Tips for Behance Presentation

How to show your work.

Tip 01: Use High-Quality Images
All screenshots should be crisp, high-resolution. At least 1200px wide. Use 2x resolution if possible. Blurry screenshots look unprofessional. Behance viewers expect high production quality.
Tip 02: Include Context & Explanation
Every slide should have a brief caption explaining what viewers are looking at and why it matters. Don't assume viewers understand the methodology. Context helps non-designers understand the thinking.
Tip 03: Show Iteration, Not Just Final
Include before & after. Show that you iterated. Show the design audit and the resulting changes. Iteration is more interesting than perfection. It demonstrates process and thinking.
Tip 04: Tell a Story
Arrange slides to tell a narrative: Problem → Research → Direction → Design → Audit → Redesign → Result. Don't just dump screenshots. Lead viewers through your thinking.
Tip 05: Highlight the Distinctive Part
What makes this project unique? For Pawside, it's the AI-assisted design process and the strategic audit methodology. Emphasize what's different, not what's expected.
Tip 06: Include Project Details
At the end, include a project details slide: Tools used (Claude, Midjourney, HTML/CSS), timeframe, team (just you or collaboration), and learnings. This metadata adds credibility.
Tip 07: Optimize for Mobile Viewing
Many Behance viewers use mobile. Ensure text is readable at small sizes. Use high contrast. Avoid tiny type or subtle colors. Mobile-first thinking improves overall presentation.
