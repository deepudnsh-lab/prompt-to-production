Step 04 — Industry Research · Brief locked
Three questions · ten words · research begins
Worked first time
Prompts sent to Claude — locking the brief
Q: What age group is Bumblebee World targeting? A: Early learners (3–6 years) Q: Who is the primary buyer persona — who actually pays? A: Parents (urban, aspirational) Q: What is the core product type? A: books and toys
What worked
Three answers, ten words total, produced a complete and correctly-framed research brief. Claude identified the key distinction immediately: the buyer is the parent, not the child. The research correctly oriented around urban Indian mothers aged 28–38. The design thesis — "Every competitor is either beautiful or Indian or educational. Bumblebee World is all three" — arrived fully formed in the first draft.
What needed fixing
Nothing significant. The research was well-sourced — ₹4,200 Cr books, ₹18,500 Cr toys, 62% gifting purchases, all named with sources and years. The competitor gap analysis was sharp. The document required no correction pass before being approved.
Lesson
The three-question brief format works across any industry. Answering who pays, who uses, and what is the product is sufficient to unlock a complete research document. Making the buyer/user distinction explicit at the outset is the single most important brief decision for a children's brand.

Step 06 — Competitor swap · Cupcake & Co.
Site unavailable · four words · complete replacement
One iteration
Prompt sent to Claude
cupcakeandco.in is not opening
What worked
Four words produced a complete competitor replacement. Claude searched and identified Tulika Books — 25+ years, multilingual, Indian, D2C webshop — as a direct comparable and rebuilt the entire audit section. Tulika's D:2 I:5 S:1 score sharpened the gap analysis more than Cupcake & Co. would have: an award-winning Indian publisher with an un-Instagrammable website is the clearest proof of Bumblebee World's territory.
What needed fixing
The swap needed to be documented transparently in the document itself — a note reading "replaces Cupcake & Co. (site unavailable at time of audit)" so a portfolio reviewer reading the document in isolation understands the change was deliberate, not an error.
Lesson
When a source becomes unavailable mid-project, document the swap explicitly in the deliverable — not just in the conversation. A portfolio reviewer reading the audit in isolation must be able to understand why the third competitor differs from the one named in the brief.

Create a professional brand moodboard for an Indian children's books and toys brand called Bumblebee World targeting early learners aged 3 to 6. What worked
All three moodboards came back usable on first generation — no regeneration needed. The bento-box grid instruction (carried from OrbitX) reliably produces a 9-panel design presentation. Naming hex codes caused Midjourney to include a colour palette strip panel. The Indian girl holding the wooden bee toy and the marigold flat lay appeared in Direction A exactly as briefed.
What needed fixing
Nothing significant on any of the three directions. The OrbitX lesson — "include the use context in Midjourney prompts" — was applied here and produced the correct professional presentation register first time, with no regeneration required.
Lesson
The bento-box grid instruction is now standard for every Midjourney moodboard prompt in this series. Name the hex codes. Describe all 9 panels. Include "like a real designer's client presentation." These three elements together consistently produce a professional moodboard, not a mood illustration.

Step 09 — Landing page · First attempt
Animated honeycomb hero · layout too conventional
Multiple iterations
Prompts sent to Claude — deciding the hero approach
Q: Do you have images to upload for the landing page sections, or should I build it with CSS illustrations and placeholder zones? A: can u use https://unsplash.com/ Q: For the hero section — what should the background be? A: Animated CSS — honeycomb pattern, floating bees, sunshine yellow
What worked
The first build correctly applied the Direction A palette, all seven sections from Step 8 copy, and the animated floating bees with honeycomb SVG pattern. The bee emoji cursor was a strong micro-interaction detail. Unsplash images loaded correctly via embedded CDN URLs.
What needed fixing
The layout felt too conventional — standard rectangular cards, predictable grid, e-commerce template with brand colours applied. The brief called for a page that attracts young kids and the first version read as a functional shop, not a world for children to enter.
Lesson
For a children's brand, the layout IS the message. A grid-based e-commerce structure communicates "shop" not "world." Visual ambition — organic shapes, wavy dividers, illustration-forward — must be declared in the brief before the build, not corrected after it.

What worked
The reference image upload was the most effective single prompt in this brand's entire build. The rebuild applied the illustration style to CSS-generated elements — but emoji as book card characters over gradients were not convincing as actual cover art. The illustration quality needed was beyond what CSS alone could produce, which eventually drove the pivot to the Gemini-built file.

Upload the reference before writing the description. For visually ambiguous briefs, a reference image replaces 200 words of written direction and is more precise. If you have a visual reference for illustration style or layout ambition, use it as the prompt — not the footnote.

Step 09 — The cloud dive experiment
Right concept · four failed implementations · correct instinct
Pivot moment
Prompt sent to Claude
No. This is, again, not looking good. Okay. Now let me rearrange the flow of the whole website. Instead of having a horizontal I mean, a vertical scroll from top to bottom. Let us create a very three d and interactive website in which we show all the content in clouds. So the the whole home page hero banner should be a plus of beautiful looking clouds. And as the user scrolls, they move deeper and deeper inside the clouds. With each section appearing from within the cloud itself. And when they scroll back up, they can reach back to the first hero section again. So the first I mean, so it instead of going down, you go deeper into the website, and each section unfolds in a very cool and very animated way. As we go deeper into the clouds. The clouds will appear and disappear as we go deeper into the website.
The concept was right
Scrolling inward through clouds — sections emerging from mist as you dive deeper — is exactly the magical, childlike interaction that separates Bumblebee World from a generic D2C site. The instinct to replace vertical scrolling with a sense of entering a world was correct. A children's brand should feel like opening a storybook, not scrolling a shop.
The execution failed four times
Four separate cloud implementations were attempted: Three.js volumetric clouds, GLSL fragment shader, CSS 3D perspective, SVG Ghibli-style. Each produced clouds that were either too heavy, static, not convincing, or visually wrong. The gap between the concept and what CSS/Canvas could deliver in a single HTML file was too wide.
Lesson
When four implementations of the same concept all fail, the concept is not achievable at the required quality within a single HTML file. Abandon the approach — not the brand. Add "is this achievable in a single self-contained HTML file?" as a feasibility question before committing to any technically complex interaction.

The multi-tool moment · a new workflow for the series

Gemini built it.
Claude fixed it.

After seven landing page iterations in Claude, a separate HTML file built in Gemini was introduced into the workflow. The Gemini file brought three things Claude's attempts had not produced: a compelling forest illustration system with parallax jungle layers (tiger, peacock, deer, butterflies) at a visual quality level Claude's CSS could not match; illustrated book cover mockups with characters specific to the Bumblebee World story universe; and a fully working dark/light theme toggle. The visual ambition was immediately higher — but the file had the wrong brand name ("Bumblebee Wild"), wrong copy, wrong colour palette (dark forest green instead of Direction A Sunny/Bloom), and AI-generated image slots requiring a Google Imagen API key to load.

The correct workflow: bring the Gemini file back to Claude, triage what is structurally right (keep), content-wrong (replace), and technically broken (fix). Replace all copy, remap the palette to Direction A, embed all five images as base64, fix responsive behaviour, and lock the dark/light theme. Neither tool alone produced the final output. The combination did.

The forest parallax illustration system — layered jungle depth with multiple illustrated elements — was significantly better than anything Claude had built. Illustrated book cards showed characters as cover art, not stock photography. The dark/light theme toggle was fully implemented. The visual quality justified bringing this file back rather than rebuilding from scratch.
What needed fixing
Brand name "Bumblebee Wild" throughout — global replacement needed. All copy wrong — different character names, different products. Palette was dark forest green, Direction B territory, not Direction A Sunny/Bloom. All images API-dependent — blank loading skeletons without a Google Imagen key. Responsiveness untested.

When importing a file from another tool, triage it immediately: structurally right (keep) / content-wrong (replace) / technically broken (fix). The Gemini file was structurally excellent and content-wrong everywhere else. Clear triage made the correction efficient rather than a full rebuild.

Image embedding — five images uploaded
Hero · three book covers · lifestyle · all base64 embedded
Worked first time
Prompt sent to Claude — with five images attached
so instead on 3 images on the hero banner iam adding 1 image lets just use 1 image to create a simple paralax effect on mouse hover, i am also adding the rest of the images please update the this html file with all these image and fix any other issues u find, check for responsiveness as well [Images uploaded: Hero Banner image.png · bumblebee-world-book-1-img.png · bumblebee-world-book-2-img.png · bumblebee-world-book-3-img.png · bumblebee-world-lifestyle-img.png]
What worked
All five images were base64-encoded and embedded in a single pass — hero (~325KB), three book covers (~216–327KB each), lifestyle (~189KB). Total ~1.3MB. The file loads instantly with no external requests, no API keys, no CDN dependencies. Mouse-hover parallax on the hero (22px max, 6% lerp for liquid feel) worked correctly on the first implementation.
What needed fixing
Book card flip animations needed refinement after embedding to ensure the full illustration was visible on hover rather than clipped. Minor — caught and corrected in the same pass alongside all responsiveness corrections.
Lesson
Base64 embedding is the correct approach for a portfolio HTML file. No CDN dependency, no broken images when shared offline, no external requests that can fail. ~2MB total weight is acceptable for a portfolio context. For production: image CDN. For Behance and local preview: self-contained.

Asking the palette audit question triggered a full systematic remap. After correction: cream background, sunshine yellow accents, leaf green for tags, marigold for CTAs — exactly Direction A. Dark mode was remapped to a deep forest green/gold variant that felt on-brand rather than generic dark mode.
What needed fixing
The dark theme required a second targeted correction pass. 12
Light theme hero text — the final fix
Hero text turning dark in light mode · one line locked it
Worked first time
Prompt sent to Claude
This looks good. I noticed that on the light theme, the hero text turns dark. On the light theme, ideally, it should be all white.
What this fixed
All hero text — headline, eyebrow, sub-copy, trust pills, secondary button — was locked to white in both themes. The issue was var(--firefly-gold) inside the hero headline remapping to a dark value in light mode. Hardcoding the gold to #FFD700 within the hero section resolved it. The hero sits over the forest illustration, so white always reads correctly.
Why it happened
The dark/light toggle remaps CSS variables globally — correct for body text and backgrounds, but wrong for elements overlaid on a fixed dark image. The hero illustration provides its own fixed dark contrast context, independent of the page theme. Elements over images need hardcoded values, not theme-variable tokens.
Lesson
Elements overlaying images must use hardcoded colour values, not CSS variable tokens. A hero section with a fixed dark illustration is not subject to theme switching — its contrast is always against the image, not the page background. Hardcode white. Never inherit it from a token that will remap.
04 — Four key lessons · apply to remaining brands

What Bumblebee World
taught the series.

Lesson 01 — On reference images
A reference image is more precise than a written description for visual direction. Upload the reference before writing the brief. Illustration style, colour temperature, and layout ambition communicate in one image what 200 words cannot match with the same precision.
Lesson 02 — On knowing when to switch tools
Seven iterations in one tool not reaching the required quality is a signal — not to try again, but to change approach. The designer's job is to find the best output, not to stay loyal to one tool. Switching to Gemini was the correct creative direction decision for this brand.
Lesson 03 — On cross-tool palette drift
Every imported file carries the aesthetic assumptions of the tool that built it. Always audit palette, brand name, and copy against the locked brief immediately after import. The layout can look correct while the brand is entirely wrong. Palette drift is silent — catch it first.
Lesson 04 — On abandoning a technically ambitious concept
Four failed implementations of the same interaction is a feasibility problem, not a prompting problem. The scroll-inward cloud dive was the right creative instinct and the wrong technical choice for a self-contained HTML file. Ask "is this achievable in a single HTML file?" before committing to any complex scroll interaction.
