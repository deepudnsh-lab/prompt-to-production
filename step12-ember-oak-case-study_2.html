01 — The brief

What this project
set out to prove.

Ember & Oak is the first of six hypothetical brands built as part of From Prompt to Production — a self-initiated portfolio series by Deepu Dinesh, Graphic Design Lead with 13 years of experience. The project demonstrates a complete, research-led web design process executed through AI direction: Claude (Anthropic) as the execution engine, the designer as the creative director and domain expert.

The brief for this brand: design a complete landing page for a premium wood-fire fine dining restaurant in Bangalore targeting urban professionals aged 35–45. The design must close the gap identified in the competitor audit — warm within dark mode, cooking method visible at hero level, scarcity explicit, reservation friction-free.

What makes this case study credible: Every research statistic cites a named source and year. Every design decision traces back to a specific research finding or persona insight. The landing page is a single, fully functional HTML file — no framework, no backend, no tooling beyond a browser. It works offline.

03 — Key design decisions

The choices that
define the brand.

Every design decision in this project traces back to a specific research finding, persona insight, or competitor gap. The four most consequential decisions are documented below — each with the step where it was made and the evidence that justified it.

Decision 01 · Step 06 — Competitor Audit
Dark mode with ember orange as the only accent — not gold, not white
The competitor audit found that all benchmark dark-mode fine dining sites used either cool blue (EMP's indigo) or no accent at all. Orange is the colour of actual fire. It is physically warm in a way that gold is not — gold reads as decorative, orange reads as heat. The decision was made at the audit stage, reinforced by the moodboard exercise, and carried through to every CTA, label, and rule in the final build. Source: Step 6 competitor audit gap analysis.
Decision 02 · Step 05 — Persona
"Reserve a Table" — not "Book Now" · unhurried over urgent
Rohit is a product person. He values considered language over e-commerce urgency. "Book Now" signals pressure and availability anxiety — neither of which Ember & Oak wants to project. "Reserve a Table" is quieter and more possessive — it implies the table is waiting, not disappearing. This single CTA decision changes the emotional register of every conversion moment on the page. Source: Step 5 persona — trust triggers and pain points.
Decision 03 · Step 04 — Research
Named wood per dish — at hero level, not in the menu footnote
Industry research showed that fine dining websites focus on the output (the plated dish) rather than the method (the heat source, the wood, the time). Noma is the only exception — their foraging philosophy is structurally central. The decision to name Hickory, Apple, Cedar, and Camphor at the hero strip was made at the research stage. It was then carried into every section: the philosophy copy, each menu item's wood tag, and the footer closing line. Source: Step 4 research — Key Design Insight 03.
Decision 04 · Step 04 & 05 — Research & Persona
"28 guests each evening" — explicit scarcity at the first viewport
OpenTable data (2025) shows an 11% increase in group dining bookings — evidence of growing demand for curated, bookable experiences. Rohit specifically responds to scarcity signals that feel factual rather than manufactured. The decision to place "28 guests each evening" in the hero eyebrow — not below the fold, not in the footer — was deliberate. It converts browsers into bookers by introducing genuine urgency without pressure language. Source: Step 4 research + Step 5 persona motivation analysis.

05 — What this demonstrates

The skill being
shown.

01
Research-led design — every decision has a source
Nothing in this project was decided by taste alone. The dark palette came from the competitor audit. The scarcity signal came from OpenTable data. The wood-naming convention came from the research insight that cooking method is the primary visual gap in the category. A designer who can read research and translate it into specific design decisions is more valuable than one who produces beautiful work without knowing why.
02
AI as a design tool — direction is the skill, execution is the output
Claude executed 11 documents, hundreds of lines of CSS, a complete copywriting system, and a production-quality landing page. The designer chose the industries, selected the brand name, chose Direction A over B and C, approved or rejected every word of copy, and caught the process integrity errors that would have made the case study indefensible. The prompts in Step 10 are the portfolio — they show a designer thinking, not a tool performing.
03
Cross-discipline fluency — strategy, copy, visual, and code in one process
This project moves from market research to brand naming to moodboarding to copywriting to layout to a functioning HTML page. Each discipline informed the next. The copy rules were set before the layout was designed. The layout was chosen to serve the copy. The visual identity was established before either. A design lead who can hold all of these in sequence — without losing the thread between them — produces more coherent work than one who operates in a single discipline.
04
Hypothetical but defensible — every claim can be answered in an interview
Ember & Oak does not exist. But the market it sits in does. The persona it targets does. The competitor landscape it navigates does. Every statistic has a named source. Every design decision has a documented rationale. If a hiring manager asks why the CTA says "Reserve a Table" and not "Book Now," the answer is in Step 5. If they ask why the wood type is named per dish, the answer is in Step 4. The case study is bulletproof because the process was honest.
