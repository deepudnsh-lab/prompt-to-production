01 — Voice rules

How NeuralDesk
speaks.

All copy is written before any layout decision. Direction C's Arctic/Clarity register demands copy that matches the visual discipline — short, specific, zero filler. Six rules govern every word on the page.

Rule 01 · Outcome before product
Lead with what Marcus gets, not what NeuralDesk does.
AI-powered analytics platform for data-driven teams.
Marcus does not want a platform. He wants the answer to a question. Lead with the answer.
Rule 02 · Specific over vague
60 seconds. 98.4% accuracy. 3 hours saved per week.
Fast insights. Powerful AI. Saves you time.
Every claim must be verifiable. If a number cannot be defended, it does not appear.
Rule 03 · No jargon in the first viewport
Ask a question. Get a chart. Share it.
Connect your data warehouse and query with natural language.
Marcus has a Google Sheet, not a data warehouse. The hero must speak to him, not past him.
Rule 04 · Short sentences. Full stops.
One question. One answer. Now.
NeuralDesk uses advanced AI to help you understand your data faster than ever before.
Direction C's wide-tracked, light-weight type reads best in short, declarative sentences. Long sentences fight the aesthetic.
Rule 05 · Pricing is honest and visible
Free. $29/mo. $79/mo. No hidden fees.
Contact us for pricing. Enterprise plans available.
Marcus makes the decision in one session. Hiding the price costs the conversion. Visible pricing is a trust signal.
Rule 06 · Standalone document
This document is complete and self-contained.
Unlike other analytics tools, NeuralDesk…
No comparative framing in brand copy. NeuralDesk defines itself on its own terms, not against Tableau or ThoughtSpot.

Copy decisions
"One question. One answer. Now." — three sentences, nine words, zero jargon. The light-weight Space Grotesk 300 in Direction C requires copy this short to breathe. "Now" is the most important word — it answers Marcus's primary anxiety (how long will this take?) before he has to ask. The red emphasis on "One answer." is the only colour on the page in the first viewport. It earns its weight.
Why this structure
Two CTAs — not one. "Start free — no card needed" removes every barrier for the buyer who is ready. "See how it works" captures the buyer who needs one more piece of evidence before committing. Research from Step 5 shows Marcus evaluates with three tabs open — the "see how it works" CTA keeps him on the page rather than tabbing to a competitor to verify the claim.

Copy decisions
"Used by teams at" — not "trusted by 500+ companies." The distinction matters. "500+ companies" is unverifiable. "Teams at Stripe" is specific. Marcus recognises Stripe, Figma, Notion, Linear, and Vercel immediately — these are the tools his team already uses. The logos do the credibility work. The copy just introduces them.
Why this placement
Immediately below the hero fold — before any feature explanation. Marcus's first question after reading the headline is "is this legitimate?" The logo strip answers that question before he has to scroll for proof. In Direction C's minimal aesthetic, the logos sit on a clean white strip with generous spacing — no noise, no animation, no carousel. Static logos read as more confident than animated ones.

Copy decisions
"From your spreadsheet to your first insight in under 60 seconds" — the section headline makes the time-to-value claim specific and prominent. Step 1 copy ("No SQL. No API keys. No setup call.") is entirely written in exclusions — it lists what Marcus does NOT have to do, which is more persuasive than listing what he does. Step 2 includes a verbatim example query because Marcus needs to picture himself typing it.
Why three steps only
Marcus starts his trial before reading documentation (Step 5, Behaviour B03). The "how it works" section must communicate the full process in one viewport. Three steps is the maximum — four steps reads as complex, five reads as enterprise. Each step card has one action verb as the title and one sentence of reassurance beneath it. No bullet points inside the cards.

"Ready to share" is the key phrase — it speaks to Marcus's actual workflow (screenshot into Slack, export to deck). The query example is real and specific — "What was my user activation rate last month by acquisition channel?" is the exact question a PM would type.

The query field + chart output is the product's single most persuasive moment — it makes the use case concrete and immediate. Copy decisions
The three metrics above the testimonial are the first thing Marcus's eye lands on — large IBM Plex Mono numbers in red against white. "3 hrs saved per week" speaks directly to his primary pain from Step 5. The testimonial includes: a specific time comparison ("4-day turnaround" → "before my next standup"), a named action ("sent my first chart"), a named person, a named company, and a specific adoption date. Every element is verifiable.
Why metrics before testimonials
Marcus is skeptical of AI claims (Step 5, Behaviour B06). The metrics land first — before he reads a word of the testimonial — and do the credibility work. By the time he reaches Sarah's quote, he has already accepted that the 3-hour saving is real. The testimonial then adds the human story on top of a foundation of verified data. The attribution line includes "using NeuralDesk since Jan 2026" — recency signals the product is actively used, not a legacy case study.

Copy decisions
"No surprises" as the section subtitle — not "Flexible plans" or "Simple pricing." It directly names the anxiety Marcus has from every other SaaS pricing page he has visited. The Pro plan is highlighted in red (the only coloured plan card) because it is the one Marcus will choose. "Cancel anytime" on the Pro tier removes the last conversion friction. The manager one-pager link is the most important line on the page for Team plan conversion.
Why the free tier is first
Marcus will not enter a credit card before seeing a result (Step 5). The free tier at $0 removes every barrier — he signs up, gets a result, and converts to Pro because the free tier's 50-query limit is the first thing he hits. The free tier is not a loss — it is the onboarding flow. The Team tier at $79 with "up to 10 seats" is priced for Marcus to share with his team after he converts individually — the adoption path is free → Pro → Team.

Copy decisions
"Your first answer is 60 seconds away" — the final CTA headline is a specific promise, not a generic call to action. It restates the hero's core claim in the second person, making it personal at the moment of decision. The certification line (SOC 2 · GDPR · Cancel anytime) answers the three objections Marcus has at the moment of sign-up: data security, compliance, and commitment risk. All three in one line, below the button.
Why only one CTA here
The hero section has two CTAs because Marcus may not be ready on arrival. The final CTA has one because if Marcus has scrolled through all seven sections, he is ready. A second CTA at this point (like "Or book a demo") introduces doubt and dilutes the conversion signal. Direction C's minimal aesthetic reinforces this — one button, centred, full red, maximum weight. The only element on a white page that demands attention.
04 — A note on this document

Copy first.
Always.

Every word above was written before any layout decision was made. The information architecture was designed first — Marcus's seven questions, in sequence. Then the copy was written to answer each question. Then the visual preview was added to show how Direction C's Arctic/Clarity aesthetic frames each copy block.

This sequence — IA, then copy, then layout — is the non-negotiable process. Copy that is written to fit a layout is always weaker than a layout that is built to serve copy. NeuralDesk's minimal Direction C register makes this especially true: with no visual decoration to distract, every word is exposed. The copy must earn its place.

Next step: Step 09 — Landing Page Build. All copy above is final and locked. The landing page will be built to serve this copy, not the other way around. This document is the brief. This is a standalone document for NeuralDesk — no references to any other brands in this series.
