01 — Three genuinely different directions

Not variations.
Different worlds.

The competitor audit established that OrbitX sits between Virgin Galactic's warmth and SpaceX's precision — technically serious, humanly warm, always centred on the passenger. But "technically serious and humanly warm" can be expressed in multiple visual languages. These three directions each answer that brief differently, with distinct palettes, typographic systems, and emotional registers. They should feel like genuinely different design studios interpreted the same brief.

How to choose: Consider Arjun — 52, founder, 11pm in a hotel room on a 14" laptop. Which direction makes him lean forward? Which feels like a briefing rather than a brochure? Which earns his trust before it asks for his name? That is the direction. Below each direction is a Midjourney prompt to generate the moodboard image.

The feeling
Standing in mission control the night before launch. Everything is calibrated. The numbers are correct. The team is ready. There is no urgency — only precision and the quiet confidence of people who have done the preparation.
Who it appeals to
Arjun in full due-diligence mode. The founder who built a successful company by trusting data and process. This direction earns credibility first and emotion second. The blue communicates aerospace engineering before it communicates beauty.
The risk
It can tip from "technically credible" to "cold" if the human element is not introduced carefully. Blue Origin already owns a version of this territory. OrbitX's Cosmonaut direction must be warmer than Blue Origin without losing the engineering precision.

The feeling
Stanley Kubrick's 2001: A Space Odyssey — white interiors, black space, a single moment of profound stillness. This direction communicates that OrbitX is not selling an adventure. It is offering a confrontation with something enormous and permanent.
Who it appeals to
Arjun when he is in a philosophical frame — the late-night hotel room version of himself who is thinking about legacy. The gilt accent signals that this is not a technology company. It is a luxury one. Closer to a private bank than a rocket company.
The risk
Monochrome can feel dated if the typography and photography are not executed with complete precision. There is very little margin for error — one weak element and the direction collapses into generic premium. The gilt accent must be used with extreme restraint or it reads as decoration rather than distinction.

The feeling
The moment of orbital sunrise — the first light hitting the solar panels, the terminator line moving across the Pacific, the colours that no photograph has ever fully captured. This direction is about the unreproducible: what only the person who went will ever actually see.
Who it appeals to
Arjun when he is in an emotional frame — thinking about the story he will tell, the version of himself that returns from this. The violet palette is distinct from every competitor and communicates that OrbitX has seen beyond the standard space tourism visual vocabulary.
The risk
Violet can tip from "cosmic" to "fantasy" if the balance between the palette and the technical content is wrong. This direction requires rigorous typographic precision and technical data presented with the same confidence as the visual language — otherwise it reads as aspiration without credibility.

03 — Direction selection

Choose one.
Document why.

Run all three prompts on Midjourney. Place the resulting images in the drop zones above. Then select one direction. The selection should be based on which moodboard best answers Arjun's 60-second trust test: does this feel technically credible? Does it feel like a company I would have built? Does it feel like a briefing, not a brochure?

Selection framework · how to decide
The selected direction should feel like
the briefing before the launch.
Direction A (Cosmonaut) is the safest — closest to the market convention, hardest to get wrong, easiest for Arjun to trust immediately. Choose A if the moodboard communicates aerospace precision with genuine human warmth inside it.

Direction B (Monolith) is the most distinctive — furthest from all competitors, highest risk, highest reward. Choose B if the monochrome moodboard communicates luxury restraint rather than austerity — and if the gilt accent earns rather than decorates.

Direction C (Aurora) is the most emotionally powerful — the direction that most directly embodies the passenger transformation thesis from the audit. Choose C if the violet moodboard communicates cosmic scale with technical seriousness intact.
