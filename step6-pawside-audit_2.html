01 — Scoring framework

Five dimensions.
0–5 per dimension.

Each competitor is scored on five design dimensions. A score of 5/5 means the competitor excels at that dimension. A score of 1/5 means the competitor is weak or absent in that dimension. The five dimensions map directly to Maya's purchase journey and the Step 4 design opportunity.

D — Dog-as-hero photography

How prominently is the dog featured? Is the dog the primary visual focus, or is the product? Are the dogs real (actual customers' dogs) or styled/professional?

I — Ingredient transparency

Are ingredient percentages published on the homepage? Is the ingredient list named specifically or is it vague ("botanical blend")? How easy is it to find ingredient information?

S — Sourcing story

Does the brand explain where ingredients come from? Is there specificity ("oats from Manitoba, Canada") or vagueness ("ethically sourced botanicals")? How prominent is sourcing information?

C — Community & testimony

Does the brand feature testimonials from real dog owners? Are these customer reviews or styled testimonials? How many real voices are included?

M — Minimalist design aesthetic

Is the visual design minimal and clean, or is it busy and decorated? Does excess visual information compete with the product and content?

03 — Universal design gaps

What every competitor
is missing.

Gap 01
Ingredient percentages on homepage
Every competitor lists ingredients, but zero publish percentages on the landing page. This is the single biggest gap. Maya (and every dog owner researching products) wants to know: "What percentage of the shampoo is oat? 5%? 15%? 50%?" No competitor answers this directly on the homepage.
Gap 02
Dog-as-hero + transparency combined
The Farmer's Dog has amazing dog photos but vague ingredient info. Ollie has clear ingredient lists but no dog heroes. No brand combines high dog prominence with ingredient transparency. This is Pawside's opportunity.
Gap 03
Sourcing specificity
All competitors use vague sourcing claims ("ethically sourced," "natural"). Pawside should name the source: "Oat from Minnesota · Aloe from Arizona · Shea butter from Ghana." Specificity builds trust more than vagueness.
Gap 04
Minimalism without sacrifice
Tuft & Paw has the most minimal design (5/5 on M) but fails on dog prominence and transparency. The challenge: combine minimal design with ingredient prominence and dog stories. This is the hardest gap to solve.
04 — Competitor deep dives

What each brand
does well (and poorly).

The Farmer's Dog
D 5/5 · I 3/5 · S 2/5 · C 4/5 · M 2/5 — Total: 16/25
Strengths: Exceptional dog photography throughout the site. Every section features real customer dogs. Founder story is prominent. Community testimonials are authentic and numerous. Weaknesses: Ingredient list is recipe-forward ("beef, sweet potato, carrots") but percentages are never mentioned. Homepage is visually busy — there is a lot happening. Sourcing information is minimal. The design feels cluttered compared to newer competitors.
Ollie
D 2/5 · I 3/5 · S 4/5 · C 2/5 · M 4/5 — Total: 15/25
Strengths: Clean, minimal design. Sourcing information is detailed and transparent. Science-forward messaging (vet approval, clinical trials). Ingredient list is published and accessible. Weaknesses: Dogs are barely visible on the homepage — the brand feels more clinical than dog-centric. Community testimonials are sparse. The design is so minimal that it feels cold and corporate. Dogs feel like secondary to the science narrative.
Nom Nom
D 4/5 · I 2/5 · S 1/5 · C 3/5 · M 2/5 — Total: 12/25
Strengths: Playful, dog-centric visual identity. Good dog photography. Community feels warm and engaged. Weaknesses: Ingredient information is vague ("human-grade" but no percentages or sourcing). Homepage is playful to the point of being cluttered. Sourcing claims are marketing-speak, not specific. Design feels immature compared to competitors.
Tuft & Paw
D 2/5 · I 1/5 · S 1/5 · C 1/5 · M 5/5 — Total: 10/25
Strengths: Exceptional minimal design. Very clean, high-end aesthetic. Feels luxury and refined. Weaknesses: This is a accessories brand masquerading as a grooming brand. Ingredient information is essentially absent. Dogs are barely featured. The design is so minimal that there is no content to read. Community is absent.
Native Pet
D 3/5 · I 2/5 · S 2/5 · C 3/5 · M 2/5 — Total: 12/25
Strengths: Founder-driven narrative (founder's dog featured prominently). Supplements are well-explained. Some community testimonials included. Weaknesses: Ingredient percentages missing. Sourcing vague. Design is middle-of-the-road (not memorable in either direction). Feels underfunded compared to The Farmer's Dog and Ollie.
05 — Design opportunity for Pawside

Own the
unclaimed intersection.

The Farmer's Dog has dog prominence. Ollie has transparency. Nom Nom has warmth. Tuft & Paw has minimalism. Pawside has all four — plus ingredient percentages that none of them publish.

The design challenge: Build a landing page that combines minimal aesthetic (like Ollie, like Tuft & Paw) with dog-as-hero photography (like The Farmer's Dog) and ingredient transparency including percentages (no competitor does this). The page must feel premium and refined, but also community-driven and real.
