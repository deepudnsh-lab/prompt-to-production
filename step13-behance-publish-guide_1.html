The project
Ember & Oak is the first brand in From Prompt to Production — a self-initiated case study series demonstrating a complete web design process executed through AI direction. I am the creative director and domain expert. Claude (Anthropic) is the execution engine.

The process
11 steps. Each documented as a standalone HTML file. Industry research with sourced statistics. A persona built from real demographic data. A competitor visual audit across Canlis, Eleven Madison Park, and the India category average. Three AI-generated moodboard directions on Midjourney. Complete copywriting before any layout decision. Three layout concepts, one selected. A production-quality landing page — single HTML file, no framework, mobile-first.

The design direction
Direction A — Noir. Near-black base (#0E0A06). Ember orange (#C85A2A) as the only accent — the colour of actual fire, not gold. Cormorant Garamond Light for all display. DM Mono for all labels. The cooking method — specific wood types per dish — made visible at hero level. "28 guests each evening" at the first viewport. "Reserve a Table," not "Book Now."

The brief
Design a landing page that closes the gap no competitor closes: warm within dark mode, cooking method visible, scarcity explicit, reservation friction-free. Every design decision traces back to a specific research finding or persona insight. Nothing was decided by taste alone.

How to frame the series on Behance: Each brand is a separate Behance project. All six share the "From Prompt to Production" series name and the same document design system — so a recruiter who clicks one project immediately recognises it as part of a larger body of work. Cross-link all six projects to each other in the description. The series framing is what turns six individual projects into a portfolio statement.

The series thesis · what all six brands together demonstrate
A design lead who can direct AI across six industries, six visual registers, and six completely different design problems — with the same rigour, the same process, and the same standard of documentation each time.
What a recruiter sees
13 years of design experience applied to a new category of tool. Not "I used AI to make things faster." Rather: "I used AI to demonstrate a level of process rigour and design range that would take a team of five to produce manually — and I directed every decision."
What a design director sees
A designer who understands research, can write a brief, can direct a visual identity, can write copy, can identify a design gap, and can build the thing. The AI is the evidence that these skills exist across multiple domains — not a shortcut, a multiplier.
