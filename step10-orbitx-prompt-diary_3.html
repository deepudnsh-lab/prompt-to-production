What worked
This six-word prompt — used identically at Steps 4, 5, 6, and 7 — triggered fully complete, well-structured documents each time. The quality of the preceding context (brand brief, research data, persona) meant Claude had enough information to proceed without detailed instructions at each subsequent step. Embedding the three Midjourney-generated moodboards
Worked first time
Exact prompt — after uploading Vast.png · Silent.png · Luminous.png
here are the 3 mood board, please embed them in the document
What worked
All three images embedded correctly on the first attempt — base64 encoded and placed into the exact drop zones in the Step 7 document. The document structure had been built with drop zones in advance, so the replacement was clean. The images matched the direction descriptions and palette swatches already in the document.
What needed fixing
Nothing. The embed worked completely. The Midjourney images for directions B (Silent) and C (Luminous) were stronger than expected — particularly the Luminous direction, which produced a cinematic silhouette-at-the-porthole image that became one of the reference points for the landing page visual direction.
Lesson
Build documents with drop zones in advance. When you know images are coming, structure the HTML to receive them explicitly. The prompt to embed becomes trivial — one sentence — because the document is already waiting for the content. Prepare the container before you have the content.
03
Step 07 — Direction selection
Choosing Direction A over B and C
Key decision
Exact prompt — direction choice after reviewing all three moodboards
i want to go with direction A Vast
What worked
Eight words locked the entire visual direction for OrbitX. Direction A (Cosmonaut/Vast): deep mission blue, Barlow Condensed 900 weight, signal blue accent, aerospace precision. This single decision drove every subsequent step — the palette in the copywriting document, the colour grading of the section images, the starfield in the landing page, and the CSS filter values applied to the three uploaded photographs.
What needed fixing
Direction B (Monolith/Silent) and Direction C (Aurora/Luminous) were both strong — particularly the Luminous direction which had the most emotionally powerful imagery. The rejection of C was the harder call. The risk was that violet could tip from "cosmic" to "fantasy" — Direction A held the aerospace credibility that the Arjun persona required.
Lesson
Direction selection is the highest-leverage decision in the moodboard step. Everything downstream traces back to it. Choose against the risk the persona cannot absorb — not against the direction that is least beautiful. Arjun needed credibility first. Direction A gave him that. Direction C would have been more beautiful and less trusted.

Step 08 — Copywriting · standalone correction
The 9-word fix that removed cross-brand contamination
The 9-word fix
Exact prompt — correction sent mid-step after reviewing first draft
This is a fresh document. Do not mention anything about other brands.
What this fixed
The first copywriting draft included contrast framing — explaining OrbitX's approach by comparing it to hospitality brands and other industries. Nine words removed an entire section and refocused the document entirely on OrbitX. The copy became immediately cleaner, more confident, and more specific. The document became what it should have been from the start: a standalone OrbitX document.
Why it kept happening
Claude defaults to contextualising by contrast — explaining what something is by explaining what it is not. This is useful in research and audit documents. It is actively harmful in brand deliverables. A brand should never define itself by reference to others. The pattern appeared in the persona document too. It is a recurring tendency that must be caught at every step.
The rule for all future brands
Add "This is a standalone document. No comparative framing. No references to other brands." to every step prompt as a closing instruction. It takes 12 words to prevent a recurring error. The correction prompt is now a standard part of the process — applied to every brand from Step 3 onward.

Landing Page — IA and structure feedback
Breaking from the previous project's visual pattern
Key decision
Exact prompt — feedback on first landing page build
You are using wrong landing page again. I mean the information architecture and content placement on the landing page — they both look the same. Hero banner looks kind of the same. Break the monotony. On the hero banner, make everything centre aligned. Since this is regarding space tourism, add three d elements like rocket a space rocket specifically, or a astronaut's helmet. Maybe a satellite, an actual three d globe. So these elements in different sections can appear on scroll and user should have the capability of interacting with them slightly with the mouse. The colors, I'm fine with the colors and button style. They're looking good. Overall, dark theme is also looking good. But add these elements that will link give it more of a futuristic and well thought out landing page look.
What worked
Three specific directions landed immediately and correctly: centre-aligned hero (completely changed the visual register from hospitality to aerospace), 3D interactive objects per section (right instinct for a futuristic space tourism brand), and scroll-triggered appearances. The colour and dark theme confirmation meant those did not need to be rebuilt — only the structure and interactivity did.
What needed fixing
The Three.js geometry models — cylinder rockets, sphere globes — were not convincing. The programmatic 3D approach produced geometry that looked like a tutorial project, not a premium space tourism brand. Multiple rounds of iteration on model quality followed before the decision to abandon programmatic 3D entirely in favour of real video and photographs.
Lesson
Centre-aligned hero = aerospace. Left-aligned hero = hospitality. This single alignment decision is the fastest structural differentiator between two different brand categories. When breaking from a previous project, alignment is the first thing to change. It communicates register before the user reads a single word.
06
Landing Page — 3D model quality feedback
The decision point that changed the visual approach
Multiple iterations
Exact prompt — after reviewing the Three.js geometry models
This is still looking really bad. Do you want me to upload OBJ files for three d models, or do you want me to upload references for the type of d models I need.
What worked
The question itself was the right creative direction instinct. Rather than accepting poor-quality geometry, the prompt opened two better paths: real 3D model files (OBJ/GLB) or reference images showing the quality standard required. This is the correct response to a tool reaching its quality ceiling — escalate the input quality, not the tool complexity.
What needed fixing
Three.js geometry built from primitives — cylinders, spheres, boxes — cannot match the quality of actual 3D models at this stage of tooling. The geometry is only as good as the geometry. Even with detailed ShaderMaterial and PBR materials, a lathe-built rocket reads as a prototype. The final resolution was video + real photography — which delivered far better results faster.
Lesson
Real photography and video beats programmatic 3D every time in a portfolio context. The creative direction decision — what to show — matters more than the technical approach — how to render it. Know when to stop iterating on a tool and change the approach entirely. This prompt marked that moment correctly.
07
Landing Page — Video strategy decision
Pivoting from 3D to cinematic video
Key decision
Exact prompt — the pivot from 3D models to video
can you use a video in the landing page ?
What worked
Ten words changed the entire visual direction of the landing page. A full-viewport video hero — autoplay, muted, looped — immediately creates the cinematic quality that Three.js geometry could not. The question was the right creative instinct at exactly the right moment: after the 3D approach had been tried and found insufficient, video was the next tier of quality available.
What needed fixing
Nothing. The pivot was clean. The video approach was confirmed immediately as technically feasible and visually superior. The follow-up decision — to use real AI-generated images for the section visuals rather than videos for every section — came naturally from the same conversation, reducing complexity while maintaining visual quality.
Lesson
Short questions can be strategic decisions. "Can you use a video in the landing page?" is not a casual question — it is a creative direction pivot. The brevity of the prompt does not reduce its importance. The ten-word question produced the most impactful change to the entire landing page.
08
Landing Page — AI video prompts for sections
Generating the three section video briefs
Worked first time
Exact prompt — requesting video generation prompts for the AI tool
i will use a AI tool creating these videos, i already have the video for the hero section Give me seperate prompts to create videos for Vehicle section, passenger section and mission seciton
What worked
The three video prompts were highly specific — naming exact visual moments, colour grades, camera movements, durations, and explicit exclusions. The Vehicle prompt: rocket on pad at dusk, cool blue-white floodlights. The Passengers prompt: gold visor reflection with Earth visible, pull-back reveal. The Mission prompt: interior weightlessness, locked camera, floating objects. Specificity produced usable results.
What needed fixing
The mission/apogee prompt initially required an additional exclusion: "Interior view only. No exterior shots. No EVA." AI video tools default to the most common representation of space — which is exterior. The interior weightlessness moment required explicit instruction to rule out the default. Exclusions are as important as inclusions in AI video prompting.
Lesson
AI video prompts need three things: what to show, how to show it, and what NOT to show. The third element is the one most often omitted and most often responsible for generic outputs. Always end an AI video prompt with explicit exclusions: "No X. No Y. No Z." The model defaults to the familiar — your job is to define the specific.
09
Landing Page — Final image and video strategy
Hero video + three section images + consistent colour grading
Worked first time
Exact prompt — uploading hero video and three section images
i have added a video for the hero section use it like you suggested (full-viewport video background (rocket launch or Earth from orbit) with the headline text centred over it, dark overlay so text is readable) and instead of videos or 3d models of the other sections i will be adding suitable images use them wisely, and make sure that these image may have a different color tone on all of them, so make them the same color grading so that they have consistency
What worked
The colour grading brief was exactly right: "these images may have a different color tone on all of them, so make them the same color grading so that they have consistency." This produced a CSS filter system applied at different intensities per image — the warmest image (Vehicle, golden dusk rocket) received the most aggressive grade; the coolest (Inside the Vehicle, already blue) the gentlest. Visual consistency across three very different source photographs.
What needed fixing
The first-pass grade on the Vehicle image was too aggressive — the rocket's warm exhaust glow was completely cancelled, making it look flat. The fix: reduced hue-rotate from -35deg to -28deg to preserve a hint of warmth at the base while cooling the sky. The exhaust glow needed to survive the grade or the image lost its drama.
Lesson
When colour grading images for brand consistency, preserve at least one emotionally significant element from each source image. Grade for palette unity, not temperature uniformity. The rocket image needed its warm glow to remain — kill it and the image loses its drama. Consistency in tone, not uniformity in temperature.

The brevity is the proof of the process, not a weakness in it.
03 — Three key lessons · apply to all six brands

What OrbitX
taught the series.

Lesson 01 — On document independence
Every brand document must stand entirely alone. Never let context from one brand contaminate another. Nine words fixed it every time: "This is a fresh document. Do not mention anything about other brands." Add this as a standard closing instruction to every prompt in every brand.
Lesson 02 — On visual quality
Real photography and video beats programmatic 3D at this stage of AI tooling. Know when to change the approach entirely. The creative direction decision — what to show — matters more than the technical approach — how to render it. Ten words ("can you use a video in the landing page?") produced the most impactful change in the entire project.
Lesson 03 — On short prompts
Six words can advance a step. Ten words can change a direction. Nine words can fix a document. Brevity is not vagueness — it is precision. The shortest prompts in this project produced some of its most significant changes. Front-load the context. The prompts can then be short.
