01 — The persona

Meet Priya.
She is the brief.

The buyer of Bumblebee World is not the 3–6 year old child. Children cannot read the marketing, cannot search Google, and cannot swipe an Instagram story. The buyer is Priya — a mother aged 28–38, urban India, aspirational, educated, working. She buys the product. She decides whether it goes on the shelf or gets returned. She is the one who shares it on Instagram. Every design decision is made for her, while being experienced by her child.

02 — Six defining behaviours

How Priya actually
buys things.

01
She discovers on Instagram, not Google
Priya follows 12–15 Indian parenting and "mom aesthetic" accounts. New children's products enter her awareness through Instagram Reels and Stories — not paid ads, but organic shares from other mothers she trusts. A recommendation from @bangalore_mama carries more weight than a Google search result.
Signal: Product aesthetics must be Instagram-shareable. The packaging is marketing.
02
She researches seriously before buying
After Instagram discovery, she spends 15–25 minutes researching: reads reviews on Amazon, checks the brand's website for developmental credentials, looks for the founder's story, and texts 2–3 friends who have already bought. The impulse to buy is social. The decision to buy is rational.
Signal: The website must answer her questions quickly. Credibility signals above the fold.
03
She buys for gifting as often as for herself
62% of premium children's product purchases are gifts — birthdays, baby showers, festivals. Priya buys Bumblebee World both for Aarav and to give to her sister's 3-year-old, her colleague's newborn, her neighbour's daughter. The gift must look beautiful before it is opened, which means the unboxing experience is part of the product.
Signal: Packaging is a gifting decision. Must look premium in a photograph.
04
She is proud when the brand is culturally Indian
Priya grew up reading Amar Chitra Katha and watching Doordarshan. She wants Aarav to know about Diwali, Holi, classical Indian stories — but presented in a format that feels modern and premium, not dated. She rejects both bland Western content and low-quality "desi" content equally. She wants the third option: Indian stories with international design quality.
Signal: Indian characters and festivals, rendered with premium illustration quality.
05
She has 20 minutes, not two hours
Priya is a working mother. She manages a product team by day and Aarav's bedtime routine by night. She has a narrow purchase window — typically Saturday morning while Aarav is with her husband, or Sunday evening during his nap. The website must communicate its value proposition and earn her trust within the first scroll. She will not come back.
Signal: Value proposition visible in 8 seconds. Trust earned in the first section.
06
She responds to founder stories and visible values
Priya prefers brands where she knows who made it and why. A founder photo, a founding story, a clearly stated developmental philosophy — these convert her from interested browser to loyal buyer. She has left websites that look beautiful but feel corporate and empty. She needs to know there is a person behind the product who shares her values.
Signal: Founder-visible brand. Values stated in the hero, not buried on an About page.

04 — Trust signals & friction points

What converts her.
What loses her.

What builds trust · show these above the fold
Founder photograph and founding story — visible on homepage
Child safety certifications — BIS, CE, EN71 marks visible
Developmental benefit stated specifically — not just "educational"
Indian characters and settings — she can see her child in the story
Real parent reviews with photographs — not anonymous star ratings
Age-appropriateness stated clearly — "for 3–5 years" not "for all ages"
Premium packaging visible in product photography
What creates friction · remove these immediately
Generic stock photography of white children
No founder story — feels corporate and anonymous
Vague developmental claims — "boosts creativity" with no specifics
Slow website — she abandons after 4 seconds
No reviews or very few reviews
Unclear delivery timeline — she is often buying as a gift with a date
Packaging not visible — she needs to see what she is gifting
05 — Design implications · six decisions from the persona

What Priya tells us
to build.

Implication 01
Instagram-first visual design
Every product image, every packaging photograph, every website section must be designed for the 9:16 phone screen first. If it doesn't look beautiful as an Instagram Story, it is not finished.
Implication 02
Founder visible on the homepage
Priya converts to buyer when she knows who made it. The founder's face, name, and one-sentence reason for starting must appear on the homepage — not on a separate About page she will never visit.
Implication 03
Value proposition in 8 seconds
She has 20 minutes total. She will not scroll past the first section if the brand has not communicated its thesis. The hero must answer: what is this, who made it, and why should I trust it — in one viewport.
Implication 04
Indian children visible everywhere
Every illustration, every character, every photograph of a child using the product must feature Indian children. Priya is proud of this brand because her child can see themselves in it.
Implication 05
Gifting pathway must be obvious
62% of purchases are gifts. "Send as a gift" must be a clear option at every purchase point. Gift wrapping, gift message, and gift delivery must be highlighted — not buried in the checkout flow.
Implication 06
Two tests for every design decision
Every element must pass two simultaneous tests: the child's engagement test (is this stimulating, colourful, joyful?) and the parent's Instagram test (is this beautiful enough to share?). Both must pass. Neither alone is enough.

The Bumblebee World buyer thesis
Priya does not buy toys. She buys what kind of mother she is. A brand that looks beautiful, celebrates India, and makes her child's face light up — that is a brand she will buy again, gift to others, and tell her friends about. Design for her pride. The child's joy will follow.
